# Visual library

A tab's instrument answers one reader question, and more than one picture can answer it. The library is the set of visuals a renderer can draw a tab's instrument with, each declared once, so the renderer picks the one that fits the set it is given and the reader can switch to another that also fits. (basis: maintainer, 2026-11-19)

## What a visual declares

- **The instrument it draws.** The tab's instrument, as the page names it, drawn either as another layout of that instrument's own body, sharing its data, filters and detail panel so only the picture changes, or as a body of its own that answers the tab's question from the same store. One visual may serve more than one tab.
- **What it needs.** A test on the item store that makes it eligible, such as at least two gates. It is computed, never judged by hand, so a set that lacks the data never shows an empty picture.
- **When it wins.** A test that says it fits this set better than the visuals after it, such as most items sitting at the shallowest depths of the map. An item's depth is its column on the map: the length of the longest chain of waits leading into it, counting from 0, with an item that waits on nothing placed one column before the earliest item that waits on it.
- **Why.** One clause the page shows when the visual is drawn first, so the reader sees why this picture.

## How a tab picks

[Tab design](../../document/rules/tab-design.md) lists, under a tab's **Visuals**, its candidates in preference order. The renderer takes the first four whose needs hold, in that order, and draws first the earliest whose "wins when" holds, else the earliest eligible. With two or more eligible it shows a picker above the instrument, and the reader's choice is kept per tab on their device. A link that names one of them (`#view=<name>`) opens that view, over both; a name that isn't one is ignored. A tab with fewer than two eligible candidates, or none listed, draws its instrument alone. A page read with scripts off, or a backend that runs none, shows no picker and draws alone the one drawn first. (basis: maintainer, 2026-11-19 — a picker wherever two or more visuals fit, at most four)

## The visuals

| Visual | Instrument | Answers | Needs | Wins when | Why, as the page says it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `map-web` | map | What waits on what, and which gate does each item serve? | a gate, and a link between items | two or more gates | the set has several gates, and the web shows what clusters around each and the threads between them |
| `map-gates` | map | What must happen before each gate opens? | two or more gates | twelve or more items besides gates, three quarters of them at depth 0 or 1 | most items wait on little or nothing, so columns by depth would stack them; the gates read best in a row |
| `map-lanes` | map | What waits on what, step by step? | always | always | the work runs several steps deep, so columns by depth show the order |
| `gate-board` | map | What is each gate still waiting on? | two or more gates on the map, and an unfinished item that one of them waits on, directly or through items that aren't gates; unfinished by the map's own test (work not merged, a question or decision not agreed, recorded or answered, a dependency not closed, met or done) | never: the web and the row show the threads between items, and the board is the reader's pick | each column is one gate in rollout order, as the status strip runs them, holding what it still waits on that no earlier gate does |
| `question-ageing` | register | Which answers are late? | two or more waiting items with a first-filed date and a dated due point; waiting is a question or decision open, leaning or proposed and not restricted, and a due point that names a gate counts as that gate's opens-on date | a waiting item is past its due point, whether or not it has a filed date to draw | a waiting answer is past its due point, so the ages come first |
| `decision-cards` | register | What needs whose answer, by when? | always | always | no waiting answer is past its due point, so the cards lead with what needs whose answer |
| `status-briefing` | status | Where does the work stand, and what does it need? | always | no rollout is part-way | no rollout is part-way, so the briefing reads the whole state at once |
| `forecast` | status | Will the next gate open on its date? | a merge date on a piece of work, three or more weeks from the first merge's week through this one, and a next gate with a dated opens-on that needs at least one piece | never: a forecast is a reader's check, and the briefing or the rollout leads | the forecast shows whether the next gate's pieces merge by its date |
| `rollout-stages` | status, cutover | How far out is it, and is it healthy? | two or more gates, at least one run | a rollout is part-way: some gates have run and some haven't | the rollout is under way, so how far out it is and whether it is healthy come first |
| `since-you-last-looked` | status | What changed since my last visit? | a dated change in the store | never: it reads from the reader's own last visit, which differs per reader | it lists what changed since your last visit |
| `cutover-rail` | cutover | In what order does it go live, and can each step be undone? | always | always | no rollout is part-way, so the rail shows each gate's card in order |
| `incident-timeline` | status | How long until it was found, contained and fixed? | timeline events with a readable `at`, one in `detection` and one in `mitigation` or `resolution` | the set records the incident's outcome | the set records an incident, so how long it took to find, contain and fix comes first |
| `cause-chain` | status | What let it happen, and what answers each part? It draws the contributing factors, trigger first, each with the actions that serve it, and a factor's certainty only where the factor carries one | two or more contributing factors | a factor has no action serving it | a factor that let the incident happen has no action yet |
| `reach-rings` | status | How far does this bug reach? A fixed bug's callers, entry points, data and related inputs from the task record's reach items | an entry point among the reach items | data it reached is still wrong, or no test runs it through an entry point | data the fault reached is still wrong, or no test runs it through an entry point |
| `review-rounds` | status | Is the review converging? Each round's standing findings by grade, from the verdict its review record files | verdicts from two or more rounds | the latest round's standing findings are no fewer than the round before's, or more of them are critical or high | the latest review round found no fewer standing findings than the one before, so the review isn't converging |
| `change-footprint` | trace | Where does the change land, and do its tests run it? Each area's new lines and how many the tests run, from the test plan's coverage readings | a coverage reading with new lines measured | an area's tests run under half its new lines, on a run of the whole suite | the tests run under half the new lines of some part of the change, so where comes first |
| `trace-graph` | trace | Does every requirement have work and a check that holds? | a requirement | always | the trace from work to requirement and its checks is the tab's own view |
| `assumption-grid` | raid | Which assumption would hurt most if wrong? The open assumptions by confidence and cost if wrong, as the raid log grades them | an open assumption graded on both | an open assumption has low confidence and high cost if wrong | an open assumption is a guess the work can't afford to get wrong, so where each stands comes first |
| `raid-board` | raid | What could hit, and when? | always | always | the log's risks, issues and dependencies by when each could hit are the tab's own view |
| `before-after` | cutover | Did the change do what it meant to? Each goal and guardrail metric's latest reading against its baseline, by the direction it was meant to move | a goal or guardrail metric with a baseline, a reading and a direction | one moved against its direction, or a hold metric left its tolerance | a goal or guardrail metric moved the wrong way since its baseline, so the moves come first |
| `option-scoring` | design, adrs | Why did this approach win? A decision's options by plan's approach-scale rungs, axis by axis in its priority order, and any MUST an option failed | a decision with two or more `scores` lines, each with all five rungs or a failed MUST | a scored decision is still open, leaning or proposed | a decision weighed on the approach scale is still open, so how its options scored comes first |
| `adr-records` | adrs | What was decided, and why did the others lose? | always | always | the decision records are the tab's own view |
| `design-slices` | design | Which gate does each slice open? | always | always | no diagram marks a change and no scored decision is open, so the slices come first |
| `design-sequence` | design | What happens, step by step, across the parts? | a sequence diagram among the tab's sections that the renderer can draw from its elements | one of its participants or messages is marked added or changed | this change adds or changes steps in a flow, so the flow comes first with them marked |
| `design-states` | design, data | What states can a thing be in, and what moves it? | a state diagram among the tab's sections that the renderer can draw from its elements | one of its states or moves is marked added or changed | this change adds or changes states or moves, so the states come first with them marked |
| `data-chips` | data | What do we process, why, and how long is it kept? | always | always | no diagram on this tab marks a change, so what is processed and kept comes first |

(basis: maintainer, 2026-11-19, the web as the map's default where gates give it clusters; field findings, 2026-11-19 — a set whose questions all shared depth 0 drew as one tall strip; the bars are the smallest that set clears, and a set of a dozen items or fewer reads either way; maintainer, 2026-11-19, the gate board as the reader's pick and never first, the ages first only when an answer is late; two gates or two dated answers are the fewest a board or a comparison of ages needs; the briefing first unless a rollout is under way; three weeks of merges, the fewest a weekly pace can be read from; four full weeks for the band, about a month, recent enough to show a change of pace; a week as the default window, about one gap between visits)

**Terms the rows use.**
- A **merge date** is the date on a piece of work's evidence whose link names, as a whole number, the review request its `ships in` gives.
- A gate has **run** when its evidence holds any verification outcome.
- The **next gate** is the first gate in rollout order that hasn't run. With no dated opens-on it has no forecast, rather than one for a later gate that hides the missing date.
- A **forecast** covers the pieces the next gate needs: those it and every earlier gate are `blocked-by`.
  - The rest land at the team's pace: merges of every piece of work per full week, from the first merge's week, the running week left out.
  - The band runs from the slowest to the fastest of the last four full weeks, or of every full week when there are fewer.
- A rollout stage's **health** is each metric the gate `rests-on`, its value against its target, in the metric's direction, and the stage's own outcome.
  - A metric with no direction, or no numeric value and target, is shown unjudged.
  - A stage is healthy only when at least one metric is judged, none is off target and no outcome `fails`. With nothing judged, its health is not stated.
- A **dated change** is one of the following, each counted when it falls after the visit's day, not on it:
  - an agreement's date, for each item it settles, *withdrawn* when that settlement's content carries `withdrawn`, else *decided*;
  - an item filed after the set's first filing, which is its start, not a change;
  - a merge date;
  - a dated verification outcome `holds` or `fails`.

  Without a visit recorded on the reader's device, the list counts from the week before the latest change.

(basis: maintainer, 2026-11-19, the incident, bug and design visuals. A tab's always-eligible visual is listed last, so a visual whose "wins when" holds can come first. A visual's needs are the least that makes its picture say something: an incident timeline is drawn once something was found and something then done, since before that there is nothing to time; a cause chain from two factors, as a chain of one is a sentence. A change footprint's half is the gap large enough to name the area, read only from a run of the whole suite, since part of it gives a floor and under half there is unclear, never a bar on coverage, and a project's own coverage threshold isn't read here, since the half only orders the views. An assumption grid needs one graded assumption, its place on the grid being the claim; it colours cost first, as plan ranks the riskiest, red for a costly guess and amber for a costly assumption not yet shown or a middling guess, and leaves an assumption the code or data shows plain, since it owes no validation first. Before and after reads only goal and guardrail metrics, the ones a step exists to move or stops on, and leads only on a move the wrong way, the one a reader must act on. It judges direction, not size: any move counts, and whether it is enough is the gate's go-when, read against the target. A goal that hasn't moved is amber, not yet delivered; a guardrail that hasn't moved has held. Option scoring colours only a failed MUST and a one-way door, the settled grades staying plain; on Design it follows the diagrams, the change itself being that tab's subject, and on ADRs it leads. Reach rings colour data still wrong red, a failure to repair, and an entry point no test runs amber, untested; public is said in bold, not coloured, since exposure isn't an action. They lead only while one of those stands, the items being filed again as the fix and repair proceed, and follow review rounds on Status, a review not settling being the more urgent act. Five Status visuals can be eligible at once on a bug fixed after an incident; the picker's four then leave the briefing out, which the tab's lead still carries. A review converges when its standing findings fall and no more of them block a merge, since fewer findings of a worse grade isn't settling. An incident's durations count from its onset, or from when it was found where no onset is recorded, and the page says which; it is contained when the first mitigation has taken hold, at that event's end where it spans time, and fixed at its first resolution event. A terminal state is one no move leaves, a move into the diagram's end not counting.)

The renderer holds each visual's tests beside its drawing, and this table is what they compute.
