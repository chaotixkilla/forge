# Separate claim from inference

A report is trustworthy only when a reader can tell what a source *said* from what you *concluded*. Blur the two and the conclusion silently inherits the authority of its input — the most common way a research answer overstates what it actually knows. Cited from [frame-the-question](../phases/01-frame-the-question.md), [gather-evidence](../phases/03-gather-evidence.md), [synthesize](../phases/05-synthesize.md), and [compose-output](../phases/06-compose-output.md).

1. **Keep the two distinct on every finding.** The source's own claim (verbatim where load-bearing, with its anchor) is one thing; the inference you drew from it is another, carrying only the confidence your reasoning earns — never the source's tier.
2. **An inference stays at the strength of its weakest input.** A conclusion built from a derivative or weak-tier finding is itself weak-tier, however sound the reasoning; strength travels with the source, not with your confidence in the conclusion ([weight-by-source-strength](weight-by-source-strength.md)).
3. **Label the inference as yours in the output.** "The spec says X" and "so this behavior is probably wrong" are different statements at different confidence — state them separately, never merged into "the behavior is wrong per the spec." An assumption carried in from the question ([frame-the-question](../phases/01-frame-the-question.md)) is an inference too, and owes the same labeling.

(basis: the claim/inference separation is the anchoring discipline of authoritative sourcing; gather's [separate-provenance-from-conclusion](../../gather/rules/separate-provenance-from-conclusion.md) cites this rule as its basis.)
