# Name the uncertainty

State what you could not establish as plainly as what you could. A gap named is a gap the reader can decide about; a gap hidden behind confident prose is a trap. An honest "I could not confirm this" beats a fluent guess dressed as a finding. Cited from [synthesize](../phases/05-synthesize.md) and [compose-output](../phases/06-compose-output.md).

1. **Report the gaps as first-class content, not a footnote.** The open sub-questions, the thin-evidence dead ends, and the claims that stayed unverified belong in the answer, next to what was settled — a reader budgeting a decision needs to see the holes.
2. **Distinguish "no evidence found" from "evidence of no."** "I found nothing establishing X" and "the sources establish that X is false" are different results; never let the first quietly read as the second. Say where you looked, so absence is auditable ([gather-evidence](../phases/03-gather-evidence.md)'s documented absences).
3. **Let confidence carry the uncertainty, don't bury it.** Every claim's grade already states how sure it is ([claim-confidence-scale](claim-confidence-scale.md)); use it — an unverified or contested claim is delivered *as* unverified or contested, not upgraded to smooth the narrative.
