# Follow the leads

The best evidence is often something you didn't know to search for. A rigid query list finds what you already expected; the reference in a footnote, the dissenting reply, the unfamiliar term that keeps recurring — those reshape the question. Let what you find redirect the search. Cited from [plan-the-search](../phases/02-plan-the-search.md) and [gather-evidence](../phases/03-gather-evidence.md).

1. **Harvest leads from every source.** A cited paper, a referenced standard, a named counter-result, a term of art you hadn't queried — each is a thread to pull. Re-query the lane that owns the lead rather than stopping at the first source that mentioned it.
2. **Chase the dissent hardest.** A result that contradicts the emerging answer is worth more attention than the tenth that confirms it — it is either the flaw in your answer or the exception that bounds it. Follow it before you follow more agreement ([guard-against-confirmation](guard-against-confirmation.md)).
3. **Let a lead re-open the plan, not just the query.** When a lead reveals the question was mis-framed — the real dispute is elsewhere, a sub-question was assumed away — return to [frame-the-question](../phases/01-frame-the-question.md) and re-plan, rather than forcing new evidence into the old decomposition.
