# Placing a claim on the support scale

Every claim the report carries takes a grade on the support scale in [results-and-certainty](../../../craft/evidence/results-and-certainty.md), established, corroborated, contested or single-source, except those the standard puts on certainty. That standard defines the levels, their discriminators and anchors; this rule says how deep-research's evidence places a claim on them. Support is not likelihood: it grades the evidence for a claim, not the probability the claim states. Cited from [verify-claims](../phases/04-verify-claims.md), [synthesize](../phases/05-synthesize.md), and [compose-output](../phases/06-compose-output.md).

## The standard's terms, on the source-strength tiers

`(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-13; after GRADE and ICD 203)`

- **Vetted synthesis or stronger** is tiers 1–2 of [weight-by-source-strength](weight-by-source-strength.md): primary-authoritative and vetted-secondary.
- **Credible** is expert-attributed (tier 3) or stronger; community-anecdotal (tier 4) and weaker sources are not credible, however many agree.
- **A definitive origin** is a primary-authoritative source that is the claim's own origin, such as a standard defining its own behavior.
- **Clearly stronger**: one side's best source sits a full tier above the other side's best, and the conflict then resolves to it rather than standing. `(basis: derived from the tier ladder)`

Corroboration must attach to the specific claim, not an adjacent one ([triangulate-before-trusting](../../../craft/evidence/triangulate-before-trusting.md)).

## A claim about the organization's own system

A knowledge-base finding can answer a sub-question about the organization's own system, its code, configuration, behavior or history, rather than about the outside world. That claim takes a certainty level in the same report, beside the support-graded ones: `unverified` when it rests on the document alone, higher only as far as it was checked against the system. A claim about what one of the organization's records states, such as a policy or a decision's rationale, is on certainty too, as the standard's records case. Graded for support, the organization's records would let a document's word about the system reach established. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-10-02)`

## Grading a documented absence

Some answers are an *absence*, such as "no source establishes X" or a gap in the evidence. The levels still apply once you grade the right object: the claim about the **state of the evidence**, not the missing fact. An absence is **established** when an accountable source attests the gap (a review that states "no study exists") *and* a counter-search finds nothing that fills it ([guard-against-confirmation](../../../craft/evidence/guard-against-confirmation.md)). It reaches established by the **definitive-origin path**, not the two-origins path: the accountable review is the definitive origin of the evidence-state claim, and the counter-search supplies the condition that no credible contradiction stands. Your own null result is not an independent party, so it confirms that no contradiction stands without counting as a second origin. A bare "I found nothing", with no accountable source attesting the gap, stays **single-source**: the search is its only origin. Keep the two apart by [name-the-uncertainty](name-the-uncertainty.md)'s "no evidence found" versus "evidence of no".
