A mitigation stopped the bleeding; it did not close the incident. This phase applies the durable fix (or routes it), proves the signal has actually returned to baseline and holds there, and resolves the run to exactly one terminal outcome. Its discipline is refusing to call an incident resolved the moment the alert clears — the alert clearing is the *start* of verification, not its end.

## Apply the durable fix — or hand it off

The lasting correction is almost always a **code change**, and operate is an incident skill, not a development skill. So the durable fix routes by size:

- **Hand off** the durable fix to **develop** or **debug --fix** — with the confirmed mechanism from [diagnose-root-cause](03-diagnose-root-cause.md) and the reproduction — whenever the fix is more than a trivial, obviously-correct change. A fix that needs its own design, touches feature-sized surface, or wants the code exercised across flows is not something operate absorbs. `(basis: mirrors debug's phase-06 boundary — diagnosis/incident work does not absorb feature-sized change; the owning skill makes the durable code change with its own tests.)`
- **Carry it here only for a genuine hotfix** — the change is bounded, obviously correct, reversible, and the severity justifies fixing forward now rather than waiting for the normal path. Even then, the change goes through the owning path (develop/integrate) for its guarding test and landing; operate directs, it does not bypass the change discipline. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-11 — fix-forward is licensed at SEV1, and at SEV2 while impact is still ongoing, where waiting for the normal develop→integrate path would prolong material user impact; SEV3 always hands off. Follows the runbook's fix-forward/escalation convention where one exists ([match-the-runbook-conventions](../rules/match-the-runbook-conventions.md)), else this default.)`

Until a durable fix is in place, the incident is contained, not closed. What counts as durable turns on whether the cause is gone ([mitigate-before-diagnose](../rules/mitigate-before-diagnose.md)): a mitigation that **removed the offending change** — a rollback/revert that eliminates the cause and holds at baseline — *is* a durable fix, and the incident can resolve with any forward re-fix tracked as a follow-up; a **provisional stop-gap that leaves the cause latent** (a failover over bad code, a flag-off you'll re-enable, a rate-limit) is not, and the incident stays mitigated-but-watching until the real fix lands.

## Verify: signal back to baseline, and holding

Confirm the fix worked by the signal, not by assertion. **Baseline** is defined, not eyeballed: the user-facing SLI/symptom metric is back within its pre-incident range, and — where the signal is SLO-based — the burn rate is below threshold on *all* configured windows (not merely the instantaneous rate dipping under the paging line). `(basis: Google SRE "Alerting on SLOs" — the short window clears ~5 min after errors stop, the long window ~60 min; a single sample back under threshold is the classic false "recovered".)`

How long it must **hold** is the watch window. Under `--watch`, [watch-until-stable](../modules/watch-until-stable.md) holds the run open and re-reads until the signal stays at baseline for the signal's stability window before a resolved declaration is allowed; without `--watch`, read the signal once and report the verdict as of now — often *indeterminate* for a fresh fix, said honestly. Size the hold from the signal's own evaluation window where possible; the module carries the window definition and its routed-to-maintainer fallback.

## Resolve to one terminal outcome

Every run lands in exactly one of four outcomes. Walk the questions in order — they partition the space on primitive facts a cold executor reads off the world, not judgments:

1. **Did triage confirm a real incident?** No → **stood-down** (the [assess-and-triage](01-assess-and-triage.md) flapping/false-signal branch; no remediation). Stop.
2. **Is a durable fix in place *and* has user-facing impact ended?** (*durable fix* = the offending behavior can no longer recur through normal operation — a landed correction, **or a rollback / revert / permanent-kill that removed the offending change**; a *provisional* stop-gap that leaves the cause able to recur does not count; *impact ended* = the SLI is back within baseline). When a durable fix is **already in place** (e.g. a rollback), a code change handed to develop is the **forward re-fix follow-up**, not the incident-gating durable fix — its being unlanded does not hold the incident open. If **no durable fix is in place** — impact is held only by a provisional stop-gap, or the *sole* durable fix is handed-off and not yet landed → **mitigated-but-watching**: contained, the durable fix and/or full confirmation still owed. Stop here (the watch, and `--background`, may continue it).
3. **Did the signal hold at baseline through the watch window?** For a run that reached here (durable fix in place, impact ended): held stable through the window → **resolved**. Watched but the window closed with the signal **unsettled or too thin to judge** → **indeterminate**: never rounded up to resolved — keep watching, widen the window, or hand off the watch.

`(basis: derived, and it is the RATIFIED resolution gate — resolved = impact ended AND a durable fix in place AND baseline held. The partition is proven on three primitive facts: (a) real-incident? (b) durable-fix-in-place ∧ impact-ended? (c) held-through-window? — exhaustive and mutually exclusive: ¬a → stood-down; a ∧ ¬(b) → mitigated-but-watching; a ∧ b ∧ held → resolved; a ∧ b ∧ inconclusive → indeterminate. No run is in two: (b) and the held/inconclusive split of (c) are only asked once (b) is true, so mitigated-but-watching (¬b) and {resolved, indeterminate} (b) cannot co-occur. The two-value trap — "resolved vs not" — is avoided by carrying BOTH mitigated-but-watching (a known-incomplete state) and indeterminate (a watched-but-unconfirmed state) as distinct third/fourth values; collapsing either onto "resolved" is the miss this partition exists to prevent. The impact-ended-not-root-cause-found gate is the ratified impact-ended camp (Atlassian/PagerDuty/ITIL); the SRE/incident.io root-cause camp is the encoded fork — see below.)`

The severity from [severity-scale](../rules/severity-scale.md) sets how firm the confirmation must be: a SEV1 resolution warrants the full held-at-baseline window before declaring; a SEV3 may resolve on a shorter observation. Root-cause understanding is **not** a gate on this resolution — it is captured and tracked in [learn-and-harden](06-learn-and-harden.md) as a follow-up, because operate hands the durable code fix onward and files the prevention work rather than blocking closure on it.

**The resolution fork (non-gating).** A team may run the stricter **root-cause camp** — resolution requires the cause found *and* recurrence prevented, with mitigation as an explicitly earlier milestone (`(basis: Google SRE "Managing Incidents" + incident.io — resolution ≠ mitigation)`). Where the runbook sets that bar, honor it per [match-the-runbook-conventions](../rules/match-the-runbook-conventions.md): the incident stays open until the follow-up lands. The house default resolves on impact-ended + durable-fix + baseline and tracks the cause as a follow-up; the fork routes the choice, it does not block the run.

Done-state: the run carries exactly one terminal outcome, the verification evidence (the signal at baseline and how long it held), and — for anything short of *resolved* — what is still owed. [communicate-status](05-communicate-status.md) declares it; [learn-and-harden](06-learn-and-harden.md) captures it.
