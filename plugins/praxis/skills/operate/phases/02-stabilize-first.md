Once triage confirms a real incident, the clock is on users, and the job changes from *understanding* to *stopping the harm*. This phase restores service with the fastest safe mitigation — and holds the one discipline that separates a good stabilization from a reckless one: don't destroy the evidence you'll need to explain the incident, and don't make it worse with an irreversible "fix." operate carries the authority to mitigate here — the authority a plain investigation (debug) deliberately does not have and defers to a declared incident.

## Reach for the fastest safe, reversible mitigation

Apply [mitigate-before-diagnose](../rules/mitigate-before-diagnose.md): the question is "what restores service fastest and safely?", not "what caused this?" — the cause is [diagnose-root-cause](03-diagnose-root-cause.md)'s job, next. Prefer a **reversible** mitigation whose blast radius you can bound — roll back the recent deploy, fail over to a known-good replica, shed load, flag off the feature. Reversibility is the safety property: if the mitigation makes things worse, you can undo it. An irreversible action (a data backfill, a schema change, a forward-fix that can't be unwound) is a last resort, and warrants confirmation before you take it — the standard care for any hard-to-reverse action under pressure.

operate decides *what* to mitigate and directs it; it does not own a deploy backend. A rollback or redeploy is directed through the change/CI path (a hand to integrate/ci); a code hotfix through develop/debug; an infra action through its owner. operate's judgment is the mitigation choice; the execution runs through whoever owns the lever.

## Snapshot before you stabilize when the mitigation would erase the evidence

Before executing a mitigation that destroys volatile state, run the [preserve-evidence](../rules/preserve-evidence.md) routing check: if the action would wipe non-reproducible state (rollback/restart/failover/re-image wipe heap, in-flight requests, process state) **and** that state isn't already durable in shipped telemetry, capture it first — most-volatile-first. The cheap captures (a thread dump, a metrics snapshot, a log tail) are free insurance and are always worth the seconds; an expensive capture that would itself deepen the outage (a large heap dump at peak) is conditional on a confirmed need and a tolerable pause. When the fastest fix is evidence-neutral (shed load, scale out) or the evidence already lives in telemetry, there is no tension — just mitigate. Where you need both containment and evidence, isolate rather than terminate.

This is the genuine mitigate-vs-preserve tension, and it is routed, not sided: [mitigate-before-diagnose](../rules/mitigate-before-diagnose.md) is the default, [preserve-evidence](../rules/preserve-evidence.md) is the override with the condition that triggers it. Neither wins globally.

## Record the mitigation and its kind — and done-state

Record what you did, when, and — per [mitigate-before-diagnose](../rules/mitigate-before-diagnose.md) — **whether it is durable or provisional** (the one test: can the offending behavior recur through normal operation?). A mitigation stops the bleeding; whether it also *resolves* the incident is [remediate-and-verify](04-remediate-and-verify.md)'s call, not this phase's. A durable mitigation (a rollback / revert / permanent-kill that removed the offending change) can carry toward resolution once baseline holds; a provisional stop-gap leaves the real fix owed. Either way the run carries a *mitigated* state into diagnosis pending phase 04's resolution gate — don't pre-declare it resolved here, and don't blanket-label a durable rollback as provisional. If a mitigation is genuinely unavailable or too risky to apply safely, that is itself a state to communicate ([communicate-status](05-communicate-status.md)) and may raise severity — don't force an unsafe mitigation to avoid reporting that service is still down.

Done-state: service restored (or the best available partial mitigation applied and its limits recorded), the mitigation logged **with its kind (durable / provisional)** and any captured evidence attached, and the run carrying a *mitigated* state into diagnosis.
