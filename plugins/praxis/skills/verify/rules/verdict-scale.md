# The verdict scale

Leave the placement undefined and every executor invents one: a flow whose confirmation surface appeared but whose effect nobody confirmed holds for one run and is unsettled for the next, and the same drive clears a change for one caller while holding it for another.

The verdict answers one question: **did the running application do what the change claims, on this flow, where a user reaches it?** Each graded unit takes one result from [results-and-certainty](../../../craft/evidence/results-and-certainty.md): `holds`, `fails`, `unsettled` or `not checked`. That standard owns the words, what they mean, the asymmetry between `holds` and `fails`, and reporting each word as written; this rule owns how a drive places a unit among them. The verdict rests only on what a drive was observed to do, never on what the code implies it would do. The facts it turns on are established in [separate-defect-from-environment](../phases/04-separate-defect-from-environment.md); the results are assigned in [report-the-verdict](../phases/05-report-the-verdict.md), which also owns the roll-up from per-unit results to a run headline.

**Two terms, pinned once, because the whole partition is stated over them.**

The **graded unit** is what a result attaches to, and it composes in a fixed order: the **base unit** is one framed flow, or — under `--from-spec` — one requirement; `--as-user` then **adds** one unit per (base unit, persona) pair, one pair per persona named, since the persona pass is an added drive rather than a replacement of the base one. Under both flags together the units are therefore one per requirement plus one per requirement-and-persona pair, on one report key. Every claim below is *per unit*. A unit that was not checked never smears its result onto its neighbours, and a unit that holds never lends its result to the run. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`

A **framed step** is one of the steps the framing enumerated for that unit — [frame-the-observation](../phases/01-frame-the-observation.md) owns the granularity bar and the unit boundary — together with any unhappy-path step the drive was required to add and record there. Every "every framed step" below counts that list and no other.

## Scope, and the asymmetry

Under the standard's asymmetry, the strongest thing `holds` can mean here is *driven on these units, in this environment, and not refuted*. Word it that way in the report, and read a `holds` you receive that way.

That asymmetry is why a result is never assignable on its own. It is assignable only with its **scope triple**: the unit graded, the environment recorded when the app was stood up, and — under `--as-user` — the persona. A `holds` carries no claim about units that were not driven, about the same unit in another environment, or about the application as a whole. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`

## What each result takes in a drive

`unsettled` is a result in its own right, never a retry or pending status: a driven-but-unsettled unit is verify's most common non-happy outcome. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27; after test-execution reporting's passed / failed / blocked / not-run set)`

- **holds** *(top of scale)* — the unit was driven end-to-end through its real entry point and every framed step was observed to behave as claimed, with each claimed effect confirmed where that effect is visible.
  - *Anchor:* the claim is that a signed-out user can reset their credential. The drive requested the reset through the path a user reaches, picked the reset up at the far side of the boundary the instance's own delivery path sends across in the recorded environment (the crossing observed where it lands, per [reach-the-real-entry-point](reach-the-real-entry-point.md)'s delivery-boundary cut), set a new credential, signed in with it, and confirmed the old one no longer works. Five framed steps, all observed, each effect confirmed at the layer it shows. **holds**, scoped to that unit in that environment.
- **fails** — a genuine behavioural fault that reproducibly prevents this unit behaving as claimed was established: either the unit was driven and the fault reproduced on it, or reaching the unit was itself the claim and nothing in the served instance resolves to it. The fault need not have been *caused* by the change — a reproducible pre-existing fault on the unit's path lands here too, tagged pre-existing, because the result tracks whether the unit does what was claimed while the attribution tag carries who is answerable for it. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`
  - *Anchor (reachability was the claim):* the change's stated claim is that a reset link now exists on the signed-out surface; the surface serves, and no action on it resolves to the reset behaviour on any re-drive. No framed step ran, and the unit still **fails** — the non-resolution is the reproduction.
- **unsettled** — the unit was driven, at least in part, and the observation does not settle the claim in either direction: a framed step was never reached, an effect could not be confirmed at the layer it is visible (including where a substitution or an undetermined environment fact stands under it), a misbehaviour did not hold across re-drives, the defect-versus-environment attribution came back unresolved, or it came back as an environment fault, whose pre-repair observation is void until the fault is repaired and the flow re-driven.
- **not checked** *(bottom of scale)* — no framed step of the unit was ever exercised through its real entry point, so nothing about the claim was observed at all, and the unreachability itself is not a fault the run established: either reaching the unit was not the claim under check, or it was and the non-resolution is attributed to the environment rather than to a fault in the application. The reason is always stated: the access the run lacked, the instance that never served, the isolation that could not be provisioned.

The standard's anchors for `fails`, `unsettled` and `not checked` are drives of this same reset flow, and they calibrate those three here.

## The partition, proven on primitive facts

Do not sort a unit by which definition it resembles. Ask these questions in order — each a fact about the world rather than a judgment, with (r) asked only where (a) is no — and take the first stop they produce:

1. **(a) Was any framed step of this unit exercised through the unit's real entry point?** — a *real* entry point in the sense [reach-the-real-entry-point](reach-the-real-entry-point.md) defines, and a step reached by a substitute path does not count as exercised, so you cannot answer (a) without that rule's test for a fake drive. **Yes → (b). No → ask (r) before assigning anything.**
   - **(r) Was reaching this unit itself the claim under check, *and* is the non-resolution a property of the application as served?** Both halves are required. (i) The claim asserted that this unit is reachable — the change was to create, expose, or re-point the entry point, or to make the application serve it at all. (ii) Nothing in the running instance — its code identity confirmed by [the code-identity test](code-identity.md), since a stale instance resolves nothing either — resolves a user's action to the behaviour, as against the run merely lacking the access the path demands (a credential, device, counterparty, or network path it does not hold). Half (ii) is **not** the fault question: it separates *the application does not resolve this* from *this run cannot get there*, and who is at fault for a non-resolution is (b). **Yes → the non-resolution is the failure that enters (b), which runs the cited classification on it. No → `not checked`.** Stop. Where the claim is the unit's own reachability, trying to reach it is examining that claim, which is why this branch can end in `fails` though no framed step ran. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27; the reconciliation, derived from the standard's "exercised" covering run, driven or examined)`
2. **(b) Did the drive establish a genuine, stable behavioural fault that reproducibly prevents this unit behaving as claimed?** — established only by applying the ordered classification tests, the residual-case test, and the reproduction threshold set in [real-defect-vs-environment](real-defect-vs-environment.md); a misbehaviour that has not been through those is not an answer to (b). Read that rule's four terminal attributions straight onto (b), which is the whole mapping: *reproducible defect caused by the change* → **yes**; *pre-existing defect* → **yes**, tagged pre-existing; *environment fault* → **no** (the pre-repair observation is void, so the steps under it are unobserved and the unit falls through to (c)); *unresolved* → **no**. **Yes → `fails`** — for a unit arriving through (r), with the non-resolution as its reproduction. **No, for a unit arriving through (r) → `not checked`**, since nothing was exercised and there is no observation for (c) to count. Stop either way.
3. **(c) Was every framed step observed, with each claimed effect confirmed at the layer the effect is visible?** — *observed* in the sense pinned by [observation-over-inference](../../../craft/evidence/observation-over-inference.md), which is what stops a healthy-looking surface signal from standing in for the effect it merely implies. Two halves, both required: coverage (every framed step produced an observation entry — the label [exercise-the-flows](../phases/03-exercise-the-flows.md) assigns answers this half directly) and confirmation (each of those entries confirmed its step's claimed effect at its own layer). **Yes → `holds`. No → `unsettled`.**

**The algebra:** `¬a ∧ ¬r → not checked`; `¬a ∧ r ∧ b → fails`; `¬a ∧ r ∧ ¬b → not checked`; `a ∧ b → fails`; `a ∧ ¬b ∧ c → holds`; `a ∧ ¬b ∧ ¬c → unsettled`. Six leaves over the four results.

**Exhaustive.** Every unit the run undertook has an answer to (a), because "was anything exercised" is a fact about the run rather than an assessment of it; where (a) is no, (r) has an answer, because both its halves are facts — what the claim asserted, and what the served instance resolves. If (a) or (r) holds, (b) has an answer, because the cited classification is total: it terminates in exactly four attributions, two answering (b) yes and two no, per the mapping in (b). If (b) is no *and* (a) held, (c) has an answer, because framing enumerated the unit's steps, so "were they all observed" is countable; if (b) is no and the unit came in through (r), there is nothing to count and the leaf is `not checked`. Nothing outside.

**Mutually exclusive.** Each question is asked only when the previous one answered a specific way, so no unit reaches two leaves; the three `¬a` leaves are separated by (r) and then by (b), which is why (r) is a question in the ladder rather than an override written into the cells below. The one genuine overlap is the unit that carries *both* a reproducing fault and an unreached step; it is resolved by the order, not by narrowing a definition — (b) precedes (c), so it lands `fails`. That ordering is the asymmetry doing structural work: a named reproducing fault is conclusive, while a complete-looking pass is only ever unrefuted, so a further gap in the observation cannot change what the fault already settled. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`

## The empty unit set: `not checked`, for one of two reasons

A change with no user-reachable behaviour yields an empty unit set, and an empty set has no unit for a result to attach to. The run's result then attaches to the change's claim as a whole, and it is **`not checked`**, because nothing was driven. Its reason is one of two, chosen by the guards below, and the two must not collapse: one is a finding, the other a framing that failed. Neither competes with a per-unit result, because both are reachable only when the unit set is empty and per-unit results only when it is not; and neither carries a per-unit row, because there is no unit. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27, for the guards; the result, derived from the standard's first placement question)`

**Reason: no reachable surface** — the run traced that this change exposes nothing a user can reach, so there was nothing to drive. That is a finding, and it must never be rendered as `holds` or relayed as a green pass. Available only when **both** guards hold, and each guard closes a way the negative gets abused:

1. **The absence is a property of the change, not of the run's failure to look.** You can name what the change touches and why no access path reaches it — an internal-only path, a tooling or build-side change, a restructuring whose claim asserts no behavioural delta. "No flow was found" is a fact about the search, not about the change, and does not satisfy this. The bar that decides a traced absence from an untraced one is [frame-the-observation](../phases/01-frame-the-observation.md)'s, applied at framing time.
2. **The claim under check did not itself assert user-reachable behaviour.** A change that *claims* a reachable flow and has none does not have an empty unit set — it has a unit that exists and is unreachable, graded by (a), (r) and (b) above rather than by this section. Without this guard, this reason becomes the escape hatch for "I could not find the feature", which is the exact failure this skill exists to catch.

**Reason: reach untraced** — guard 1 fails. The run names what could not be determined and what blocked the trace (an opaque surface, a build step the trace could not follow, a dependency it could not resolve). It is emphatically not the first reason, and not `holds`.

## The adjacent-result discriminators

- **`holds` vs `unsettled`** — *completeness of the observation, and this is the load-bearing cut.* `holds` needs **every** framed step observed and **every** claimed effect confirmed at the layer it is visible. One step skipped past, inferred from a downstream signal, or reached only by a substitute path drops the unit to `unsettled`, however healthy the rest looked. "Enough" is not a step count; it is *no framed step left unobserved, and no effect standing on its own success surface*. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`
- **`fails` vs `unsettled`** — *established versus suspected fault.* `fails` needs the fault to have passed the cited classification and to have reproduced at the pinned threshold. A misbehaviour seen once, or one whose attribution came back unresolved, is `unsettled` — a suspicion, reported as one. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`
- **`not checked` vs `unsettled`** — *did the run look at all.* Any framed step exercised through the real entry point → `unsettled`; none → `not checked`. A drive that got partway and then hit a wall is `unsettled`, because it produced observations the caller can use and it warns that a partial pass is not a pass. The rejected alternative — keying `not checked` on whether the obstruction lay outside the change's reach — was dropped because it duplicates (b) and makes a partway-then-walled drive satisfy both. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`
- **`not checked` vs `fails`** — *(r), then (b) on the non-resolution.* An unreachable entry point is the trap. Where (r) holds — the claim asserted this unit is reachable and nothing in the served instance resolves to it — the absent access path is the failure that enters (b), and where (b) attributes a fault the unit `fails` with the non-resolution as its reproduction. Where either half of (r) fails — the entry point is pre-existing, or the obstruction is access this run does not hold — or where (b) attributes the non-resolution to the environment, the unit is `not checked`. Filing a change's own unreachability as `not checked` is how a feature wired to nothing ships. Route this cell through (r) and (b) with no second test, never on whether the obstruction lay inside the change's reach: the missing wiring of a feature nobody registered commonly sits in a file the change never touched. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`
- **`holds` vs `fails`** — *direction, and the two do not carry equal weight.* Where clean drives and a reproduced fault disagree, investigate the inconsistency instead of averaging it. *(Direction settled by the asymmetry above; the residual house call is the reproduction threshold, which the cited classification rule owns.)*

**Tie-break direction.** When two results both seem to fit, **the result that claims less about the unit wins, unless you can name the specific observation that earns the stronger one.** The claim-strength order is: `holds` and `fails` both assert something about the running system; `unsettled` asserts only that the run looked and did not settle it; `not checked` asserts nothing at all. So an arguable `holds` drops to `unsettled` unless you can name every framed step observed, and an arguable `fails` drops to `unsettled` unless you can name the fault, its classification, and its reproduction — which for a unit reaching `fails` through (r) is the named non-resolution of the entry point. The single exception is `holds` against `fails`, where the stronger claim wins — and that is the asymmetry rather than a loosening, since the observation that earns `fails` is by definition already named. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`

## The partition holds under the flags, by construction

Every flag acts on exactly one of three axes, and none of them is the partition's membership function:

- **which units exist** — `--flows` replaces the set framed from the change, `--from-spec` re-keys units from flows to requirements, `--as-user` adds a unit per base unit per persona. Three flags share this axis, so single-axis membership is not on its own enough for the pair `--from-spec --as-user`: what settles that pair is the composition order pinned at the top of this rule, base unit first and persona-pairs added over it, which yields one determined unit set rather than two orderings;
- **what environment is recorded** — `--sandbox`;
- **what findings accompany the results** — `--ux`.

Because (a), (r), (b) and (c) are evaluated per unit against the world, and no flag adds, removes, reorders or redefines a result, the partition holds across every combination without enumerating them: the flags change the *domain* the partition applies over and the environment attached to each result, never the partition. Two consequences worth stating, since both are places a result could otherwise go missing: under `--from-spec`, a requirement no flow could reach is a unit that exists and was not driven, graded by (r) and (b) like any unreachable unit — `fails` where the claim asserted it reachable and nothing resolves to it, `not checked` otherwise — and never absent from the report; and under `--flows`, the named list is non-empty by construction, so the empty-set result is unreachable there — a list whose every name is unresolvable is a run of units each graded by (r) and (b) — `not checked` unless (r) holds — not an empty set. `--from-spec` reaches an empty set only where the spec states no requirement about running behaviour at all, which passes through guard 1 like any other traced absence; a spec full of requirements none of which could be driven is, again, a run of units each `not checked` unless (r) holds.

The degrade postures do need enumerating, because those are the states where a unit can genuinely fall outside the set:

- **The app never becomes reachable** — nothing serves, so no unit was exercised: **every unit `not checked`**, with the reason recorded — *unless* (r) holds for those units, the claim having asserted that the application serves them and the non-serving being a property of the instance as built rather than of the run's access, in which case the failure to serve is the observed fault and they `fail` on (b) through (r), the non-serving instance being the reproduction.
- **One unit's access path cannot be exercised** while the app serves others — **that unit `not checked`**, its neighbours graded on their own drives. Per-unit independence is what makes this safe.
- **Requested isolation cannot be provisioned** (`--sandbox`) — the run stops rather than driving against real state, so nothing was exercised: **every framed unit `not checked`**, reason "requested isolation unavailable". Not `unsettled`, since nothing was driven, and never a silent fall back to the ambient environment.
- **A recorded substitution or a recorded `unknown` sits under a claimed effect** — a stand-in provisioned for isolation, a substitution recorded while standing the app up in the ambient environment, or an environment fact recorded as `unknown` that a framed step's claimed effect depends on — *depends on* meaning the layer at which that effect is visible is produced by, or lies on the far side of, the substituted or undetermined thing: the effect cannot be confirmed at the layer it is visible, so (c) fails: **`unsettled`**, with the substitution or the unknown named in the recorded environment. Where no framed step's claimed effect depends on it, it caps nothing and travels as a scope note. This is the cell every gap in the environment record defers to, and it is where the isolation-versus-fidelity tension actually reaches the verdict rather than only the setup.
- **A persona's access path cannot be exercised** (`--as-user`) — that flow-and-persona unit is **`not checked`**, reason the persona's access path. It is a unit the run undertook and could not drive, so it takes a result rather than being dropped to a footnote, and it does not move the base unit's result, which was driven and graded in its own right.
- **Friction, at any intensity** (`--ux`, `--as-user`) — findings, never a result. A functioning-but-confusing unit `holds`, plus findings. The one place friction meets the partition is when it is total, and then it is not friction: a framed step the user cannot complete at all went unobserved or exposed a real fault, and the unit lands on (c) or (b) accordingly.

`(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-27)`

## What this scale does not grade

It grades one thing: whether the unit behaved as claimed when driven. It does not grade how well the change is written, whether the automated suite covers it, why a fault happens, or how urgent a fault is — a unit that `fails` carries no severity and no priority, and the caller who receives it decides those. Usability friction in particular sits on its own axis: recorded alongside the result, hunted deliberately under `--ux` and `--as-user`, and never by itself enough to move a functioning unit off `holds`.
