# The verdict scale

verify's deliverable *is* the verdict. The application it drove is left unchanged, the reproduction is an attachment, the code was never read — so the one thing a run hands back is a level per flow plus the scope that level holds within. Leave the ladder undefined and every executor invents one: a flow whose confirmation surface appeared but whose effect nobody confirmed is `works` to one run and unsettled to the next, and the same drive clears a change for one caller while holding it for another.

The verdict answers one question: **did the running application do what the change claims, on this flow, where a user reaches it?** It rests only on what a drive was observed to do, never on what the code implies it would do. The facts it turns on are established in [separate-defect-from-environment](../phases/04-separate-defect-from-environment.md); the levels are assigned in [report-the-verdict](../phases/05-report-the-verdict.md), which also owns the roll-up from per-unit levels to a run headline — that roll-up is not this rule's.

**Two terms, pinned once, because the whole partition is stated over them.**

The **graded unit** is what a level attaches to, and it composes in a fixed order: the **base unit** is one framed flow, or — under `--from-spec` — one requirement; `--as-user` then **adds** one unit per (base unit, persona) pair, one pair per persona named, since the persona pass is an added drive rather than a replacement of the base one. Under both flags together the units are therefore one per requirement plus one per requirement-and-persona pair, on one report key. Every claim below is *per unit*. A blocked unit never smears its level onto its neighbours, and a clean unit never lends its level to the run. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — base-first, persona-second composition. Because `--from-spec` and `--as-user` are the two flags that both act on *which units exist*, and nothing else orders them: applied the other way round the personas key to flows while the report keys requirements, so the tally counts a denominator the next run cannot reproduce.)`

A **framed step** is one of the steps the framing enumerated for that unit — [frame-the-observation](../phases/01-frame-the-observation.md) owns the granularity bar and the unit boundary — together with any unhappy-path step the drive was required to add and record there. Every "every framed step" below counts that list and no other.

## The asymmetry that shapes the ladder

The two directions are not equally reachable. One reproducing failure settles the question — the claim is refuted, and no amount of further driving un-refutes it. No number of clean drives settles the other direction, because the next flow, the next input, or the next environment can still fail. So `defective` is terminal and `works` is provisional: the strongest thing `works` can mean is *driven on these units, in this environment, and not refuted*. Word it that way in the report, and read a `works` you receive that way. `(basis: Dijkstra — "program testing can be used to show the presence of bugs, but never to show their absence" (The Humble Programmer, 1972); the same asymmetry is the first of the ISTQB Foundation testing principles, "testing shows the presence of defects, not their absence". This is authority-settled and not a house call: it fixes the direction of the works/defective tie-break below.)`

That asymmetry is why a level is never assignable on its own. It is assignable only with its **scope triple**: the unit graded, the environment recorded when the app was stood up, and — under `--as-user` — the persona. A `works` verdict carries no claim about units that were not driven, about the same unit in another environment, or about the application as a whole; strip the triple off and the level overstates itself the moment it leaves the run. Report the four names verbatim, too. Paraphrasing them into a pass/fail pair silently pushes `indeterminate` and `blocked` to one pole or the other, which is exactly how a run that established nothing comes to read as a clearance. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — a level is reported only with its scope triple attached, and the four names are relayed unparaphrased. Because both defects are silent: a bare "works" is indistinguishable from a global claim, and a collapsed pass/fail hides the two rungs that assert nothing.)`

## The four levels

`(basis: the vocabulary is corroborated community practice — test-execution reporting converges on a four-status set (passed / failed / blocked / not-run) in which "blocked" means the case could not be executed, so `works` / `defective` / `blocked` are not inventions. But that practice standardizes neither the labels nor the boundaries, and it has no rung for *driven but unsettled* — it tends to fold that into a retry or pending state, which is the collapse this scale must not make. So `indeterminate` and every boundary below are this build's derivation, routed on each cut.)` `(basis: ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-27 — four levels, this vocabulary, with `indeterminate` carried as a first-class rung rather than a pending status. Because a driven-but-unsettled unit is verify's most common non-happy outcome (a step never reached, an effect not confirmable, an unstable misbehaviour) and folding it into either assertion is the divergence this whole rule exists to prevent.)`

- **works** *(top of scale)* — the unit was driven end-to-end through its real entry point and every framed step was observed to behave as claimed, with each claimed effect confirmed where that effect is visible.
  - *Anchor:* the claim is that a signed-out user can reset their credential. The drive requested the reset through the path a user reaches, picked the reset up at the far side of the boundary the instance's own delivery path sends across in the recorded environment (the crossing observed where it lands, per [reach-the-real-entry-point](reach-the-real-entry-point.md)'s delivery-boundary cut), set a new credential, signed in with it, and confirmed the old one no longer works. Five framed steps, all observed, each effect confirmed at the layer it shows. **works**, scoped to that unit in that environment.
- **defective** — a genuine behavioural fault that reproducibly prevents this unit behaving as claimed was established: either the unit was driven and the fault reproduced on it, or reaching the unit was itself the claim and nothing in the served instance resolves to it. The fault need not have been *caused* by the change — a reproducible pre-existing fault on the unit's path lands here too, tagged pre-existing, because the level tracks whether the unit does what was claimed while the attribution tag carries who is answerable for it. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — level tracks conformance, tag tracks answerability, so the cited classification's *reproducible defect caused by the change* and *pre-existing defect* both answer (b) yes. Because a unit that reproducibly fails has refuted its claim whoever is answerable, and grading it `indeterminate` — "the run looked and did not settle it" — is false of a run that settled it; the tag is what stops this change being blamed for it.)`
  - *Anchor:* the same unit — the new credential is accepted by the form, and signing in with it then fails on every one of the re-drives, while the pre-change instance completes the same flow. **defective**, with the reproduction attached.
  - *Anchor (reachability was the claim):* the change's stated claim is that a reset link now exists on the signed-out surface; the surface serves, and no action on it resolves to the reset behaviour on any re-drive. Nothing was driven, and the unit is still **defective** — the non-resolution is the reproduction.
- **indeterminate** — the unit was driven, at least in part, and the observation does not settle the claim in either direction: a framed step was never reached, an effect could not be confirmed at the layer it is visible (including where a substitution or an undetermined environment fact stands under it), a misbehaviour did not hold across re-drives, the defect-versus-environment classification could not be settled, or it *was* settled as an environment fault, whose pre-repair observation is void until the fault is repaired and the flow re-driven.
  - *Anchor:* the same unit completes as far as the confirmation surface, but the reset never arrives through the recorded environment's delivery path, so the "old credential no longer works" step is never reached — everything the drive did touch looked healthy. **indeterminate** — the level cannot be `works`, because a framed step that was never reached has no observation to grade, and the healthy steps around it grade only themselves.
- **blocked** *(bottom of scale)* — no framed step of the unit was ever exercised through its real entry point, so nothing about the claim was observed at all, and the unreachability itself is not a fault the run established: either reaching the unit was not the claim under check, or it was and the non-resolution attributed to the environment rather than to a fault in the application.
  - *Anchor:* the instance stood up but the run cannot obtain the access credential the environment requires, so not one framed step of any flow was driven. **blocked** — never `works`, because nothing was seen, and never `defective`, because nothing about the change's behaviour was established.

## The partition, proven on primitive facts

Do not sort a unit by which definition it resembles. Ask these questions in order — each a fact about the world rather than a judgment, with (r) asked only where (a) is no — and take the first stop they produce:

1. **(a) Was any framed step of this unit exercised through the unit's real entry point?** — a *real* entry point in the sense [reach-the-real-entry-point](reach-the-real-entry-point.md) defines, and a step reached by a substitute path does not count as exercised, so you cannot answer (a) without that rule's test for a fake drive. **Yes → (b). No → ask (r) before assigning anything.**
   - **(r) Was reaching this unit itself the claim under check, *and* is the non-resolution a property of the application as served?** Both halves are required. (i) The claim asserted that this unit is reachable — the change was to create, expose, or re-point the entry point, or to make the application serve it at all. (ii) Nothing in the running instance — its code identity confirmed by the code-identity test in [stand-the-app-up](../phases/02-stand-the-app-up.md), since a stale instance resolves nothing either — resolves a user's action to the behaviour, as against the run merely lacking the access the path demands (a credential, device, counterparty, or network path it does not hold). Half (ii) is **not** the fault question: it separates *the application does not resolve this* from *this run cannot get there*, and who is at fault for a non-resolution is (b). **Yes → the non-resolution is the failure that enters (b), which runs the cited classification on it. No → `blocked`.** Stop. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — (a) routes through (r) instead of answering `blocked` unconditionally. Because a change whose own claim was reachability, filed `blocked`, is exactly how a feature wired to nothing ships under a clean-looking report; and the split is what makes the algebra total rather than contradicted by two cells below (unreachable entry point, non-serving instance), which now route *through* (r) instead of overriding it.)`
2. **(b) Did the drive establish a genuine, stable behavioural fault that reproducibly prevents this unit behaving as claimed?** — established only by applying the ordered classification tests, the residual-case test, and the reproduction threshold set in [real-defect-vs-environment](real-defect-vs-environment.md); a misbehaviour that has not been through those is not an answer to (b). Read that rule's four terminal attributions straight onto (b), which is the whole mapping: *reproducible defect caused by the change* → **yes**; *pre-existing defect* → **yes**, tagged pre-existing; *environment fault* → **no** (the pre-repair observation is void, so the steps under it are unobserved and the unit falls through to (c)); *not established* → **no**. **Yes → `defective`** — for a unit arriving through (r), with the non-resolution as its reproduction. **No, for a unit arriving through (r) → `blocked`**, since nothing was exercised and there is no observation for (c) to count. Stop either way.
3. **(c) Was every framed step observed, with each claimed effect confirmed at the layer the effect is visible?** — *observed* in the sense pinned by [observation-over-inference](observation-over-inference.md), which is what stops a healthy-looking surface signal from standing in for the effect it merely implies. Two halves, both required: coverage (every framed step produced an observation entry — the label [exercise-the-flows](../phases/03-exercise-the-flows.md) assigns answers this half directly) and confirmation (each of those entries confirmed its step's claimed effect at its own layer). **Yes → `works`. No → `indeterminate`.**

**The algebra:** `¬a ∧ ¬r → blocked`; `¬a ∧ r ∧ b → defective`; `¬a ∧ r ∧ ¬b → blocked`; `a ∧ b → defective`; `a ∧ ¬b ∧ c → works`; `a ∧ ¬b ∧ ¬c → indeterminate`. Six leaves over the four levels.

**Exhaustive.** Every unit the run undertook has an answer to (a), because "was anything exercised" is a fact about the run rather than an assessment of it; where (a) is no, (r) has an answer, because both its halves are facts — what the claim asserted, and what the served instance resolves. If (a) or (r) holds, (b) has an answer, because the cited classification is total: it terminates in exactly four attributions, two answering (b) yes and two no, per the mapping in (b). If (b) is no *and* (a) held, (c) has an answer, because framing enumerated the unit's steps, so "were they all observed" is countable; if (b) is no and the unit came in through (r), there is nothing to count and the leaf is `blocked`. Nothing outside.

**Mutually exclusive.** Each question is asked only when the previous one answered a specific way, so no unit reaches two leaves; the three `¬a` leaves are separated by (r) and then by (b), which is why (r) is a question in the ladder rather than an override written into the cells below. The one genuine overlap is the unit that carries *both* a reproducing fault and an unreached step; it is resolved by the order, not by narrowing a definition — (b) precedes (c), so it lands `defective`. That ordering is the asymmetry doing structural work: a named reproducing fault is conclusive, while a complete-looking pass is only ever unrefuted, so a further gap in the observation cannot change what the fault already settled. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — (b) is evaluated before (c), so an established fault dominates an incomplete observation. Because the alternative order lets an executor downgrade a real, reproduced defect to `indeterminate` by pointing at some unrelated step it also failed to reach, which buries the one finding the run did establish.)`

**The two-value trap, avoided deliberately.** A works/defective binary has nowhere to put the unit that was never driven or the unit that was driven and settled nothing, and forces both onto an assertion neither earned — `defective` inventing a fault, or `works` earned by not looking. `blocked` and `indeterminate` exist to hold those runs, and the boundary between them is mechanical rather than evaluative: it is (a), *did the run look at all*. Neither is keyed on how environmental an obstruction *feels* — that would overlap (b) and re-open the same cut every round; (r) instead asks two facts, what the claim asserted and whether the served instance resolves the action at all.

## The empty unit set: two run-level terminal outcomes

A change with no user-reachable behaviour yields an empty unit set, and an empty set takes no level — there is nothing for one to attach to. It resolves instead to one of two **run-level terminal outcomes**, chosen by the guards below. Neither is a level and neither ever competes with the four, because both are reachable only when the unit set is empty and the four are reachable only when it is not; and neither carries a per-unit row, because there is no unit.

**`no-observable-surface`** — the run positively established that this change exposes nothing a user can reach, so there was nothing to drive. That is a finding, and it must never be rendered as `works` or relayed as a green pass. Available only when **both** guards hold, and each guard closes a way the negative rung gets abused:

1. **The absence is a property of the change, not of the run's failure to look.** You can name what the change touches and why no access path reaches it — an internal-only path, a tooling or build-side change, a restructuring whose claim asserts no behavioural delta. "No flow was found" is a fact about the search, not about the change, and does not satisfy this. The evidence bar that decides *established* from *not established* is [frame-the-observation](../phases/01-frame-the-observation.md)'s, applied at framing time.
2. **The claim under check did not itself assert user-reachable behaviour.** A change that *claims* a reachable flow and has none does not have an empty unit set — it has a unit that exists and is unreachable, graded by (a), (r) and (b) above rather than by this section. Without this guard, `no-observable-surface` becomes the escape hatch for "I could not find the feature", which is the exact failure this skill exists to catch.

**`framing-unestablished`** — guard 1 fails. The run reports this outcome, naming what could not be determined and what blocked the trace (an opaque surface, a build step the trace could not follow, a dependency it could not resolve). It carries no level, because there is no unit to grade and no observation to grade it on, and it is emphatically neither `no-observable-surface` nor `works`.

`(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — the confirmed empty set is `no-observable-surface`, gated on both guards; the unestablished empty set is a *second* run-level terminal outcome, `framing-unestablished`. Because a *confirmed* nothing-to-verify and a *never-established* nothing are opposite epistemic states that one rung would make indistinguishable, and the collapse always runs from the honest report onto the flattering one — while grading the unestablished case `indeterminate` instead would hang a per-unit level on a run that has no unit, which is the other way the same distinction gets lost.)`

## The adjacent-level discriminators

- **`works` vs `indeterminate`** — *completeness of the observation, and this is the load-bearing cut.* `works` needs **every** framed step observed and **every** claimed effect confirmed at the layer it is visible. One step skipped past, inferred from a downstream signal, or reached only by a substitute path drops the unit to `indeterminate`, however healthy the rest looked. "Enough" is not a step count; it is *no framed step left unobserved, and no effect standing on its own success surface*. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — a single unobserved framed step or unconfirmed effect is sufficient to bar `works`, with no tolerance. Because any tolerance is the crack the whole scale leaks through: a run that could not reach the last step is precisely the run most tempted to call the flow good, and this is where two cold executors diverge hardest.)`
- **`defective` vs `indeterminate`** — *established versus suspected fault.* `defective` needs the fault to have passed the cited classification and to have reproduced at the pinned threshold. A misbehaviour seen once, or one whose defect-versus-environment call is unsettled, is `indeterminate` — a suspicion, reported as one. Absence of stability is not evidence of a defect any more than absence of failure is evidence of correctness. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — an unreproduced or unclassified misbehaviour is `indeterminate`, never `defective`. Because a suspicion promoted to a defect sends someone after a bug that may not exist and burns the verdict's credibility; the threshold and the classification tests themselves belong to the cited rule, not here.)`
- **`blocked` vs `indeterminate`** — *did the run look at all.* Any framed step exercised through the real entry point → `indeterminate`; none → `blocked`. A drive that got partway and then hit a wall is `indeterminate`, because it produced observations the caller can use and it warns that a partial pass is not a pass. The rejected alternative — keying `blocked` on whether the obstruction lay outside the change's reach — was dropped because it duplicates (b) and makes a partway-then-walled drive satisfy both rungs. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-27 — the cut is "was any framed step exercised", a mechanical fact, not "who owns the obstruction", because the fault-keyed alternative overlaps `defective` and re-opens the partition every round. The one override stands: where the change itself claimed the flow would be reachable, an unreachable flow is `defective`, not `blocked` — a feature wired to nothing is the change's defect, not the run's obstruction.)`
- **`blocked` vs `defective`** — *(r), then (b) on the non-resolution.* An unreachable entry point is the trap. Where (r) holds — the claim asserted this unit is reachable and nothing in the served instance resolves to it — the absent access path is the failure that enters (b), and where (b) attributes a fault the unit is `defective` with the non-resolution as its reproduction. Where either half of (r) fails — the entry point is pre-existing, or the obstruction is access this run does not hold — or where (b) attributes the non-resolution to the environment, the unit is `blocked`. Filing a change's own unreachability as `blocked` is how a feature wired to nothing ships. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — this cell routes on (r) above and on no second test stated here. Because two wordings for the trap verify most exists to catch is how the cell gets decided two ways: keying it instead on whether the obstruction lay "inside the change's reach" answers differently from (r) whenever the missing wiring sits in a file the change never touched — which is the common shape of a feature nobody registered.)`
- **`works` vs `defective`** — *direction, and the two do not carry equal weight.* Both require named observations, but they are not symmetric: `defective` is terminal, `works` is unrefuted. Never round a set of clean drives up to a proof, and never let a clean re-drive retire a fault that already reproduced — investigate the inconsistency instead of averaging it. *(Direction settled by the asymmetry above; the residual house call is the reproduction threshold, which the cited classification rule owns.)*

**Tie-break direction.** When two levels both seem to fit, **the level that claims less about the unit wins, unless you can name the specific observation that earns the stronger one.** The claim-strength order is: `works` and `defective` both assert something about the running system; `indeterminate` asserts only that the run looked and did not settle it; `blocked` asserts nothing at all. So an arguable `works` drops to `indeterminate` unless you can name every framed step observed, and an arguable `defective` drops to `indeterminate` unless you can name the fault, its classification, and its reproduction — which for a unit reaching `defective` through (r) is the named non-resolution of the entry point. The single exception is `works` against `defective`, where the stronger claim wins — and that is the asymmetry rather than a loosening, since `defective`'s earning observation is by definition already named. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-27 — weaker-claim-wins under doubt, with the one asymmetry exception. Because overstating an unsettled run is the failure this scale exists to prevent: it lands a change on evidence nobody gathered, and unlike an over-cautious `indeterminate` it is invisible downstream.)`

## The partition holds under the flags, by construction

Every flag acts on exactly one of three axes, and none of them is the partition's membership function:

- **which units exist** — `--flows` replaces the set framed from the change, `--from-spec` re-keys units from flows to requirements, `--as-user` adds a unit per base unit per persona. Three flags share this axis, so single-axis membership is not on its own enough for the pair `--from-spec --as-user`: what settles that pair is the composition order pinned at the top of this rule, base unit first and persona-pairs added over it, which yields one determined unit set rather than two orderings;
- **what environment is recorded** — `--sandbox`;
- **what findings accompany the levels** — `--ux`.

Because (a), (r), (b) and (c) are evaluated per unit against the world, and no flag adds, removes, reorders or redefines a level, the partition holds across every combination without enumerating them: the flags change the *domain* the partition applies over and the environment attached to each level, never the partition. Two consequences worth stating, since both are places a level could otherwise go missing: under `--from-spec`, a requirement no flow could reach is a unit that exists and was not driven, so it is `blocked` — unobserved, in the report, rather than absent from it; and under `--flows`, the named list is non-empty by construction, so `no-observable-surface` is unreachable there — a list whose every name is unresolvable is an all-`blocked` run, not an empty set. `--from-spec` reaches an empty set only where the spec states no requirement about running behaviour at all, which passes through guard 1 like any other confirmed absence; a spec full of requirements none of which could be driven is, again, an all-`blocked` run.

The degrade postures do need enumerating, because those are the states where a unit can genuinely fall outside the set:

- **The app never becomes reachable** — nothing serves, so no unit was exercised: **every unit `blocked`**, with the reason recorded — *unless* (r) holds for those units, the claim having asserted that the application serves them and the non-serving being a property of the instance as built rather than of the run's access, in which case the failure to serve is the observed fault and they are `defective` on (b) through (r), the non-serving instance being the reproduction.
- **One unit's access path cannot be exercised** while the app serves others — **that unit `blocked`**, its neighbours graded on their own drives. Per-unit independence is what makes this safe.
- **Requested isolation cannot be provisioned** (`--sandbox`) — the run stops rather than driving against real state, so nothing was exercised: **every framed unit `blocked`**, reason "requested isolation unavailable". Not `indeterminate`, since nothing was driven, and never a silent fall back to the ambient environment.
- **A recorded substitution or a recorded `unknown` sits under a claimed effect** — a stand-in provisioned for isolation, a substitution recorded while standing the app up in the ambient environment, or an environment fact recorded as `unknown` that a framed step's claimed effect depends on — *depends on* meaning the layer at which that effect is visible is produced by, or lies on the far side of, the substituted or undetermined thing: the effect cannot be confirmed at the layer it is visible, so (c) fails: **`indeterminate`**, with the substitution or the unknown named in the recorded environment. Where no framed step's claimed effect depends on it, it caps nothing and travels as a scope note. This is the cell every gap in the environment record defers to, and it is where the isolation-versus-fidelity tension actually reaches the verdict rather than only the setup.
- **A persona's access path cannot be exercised** (`--as-user`) — that flow-and-persona unit is **`blocked`**. It is a unit the run undertook and could not drive, so it takes a level rather than being dropped to a footnote, and it does not move the base unit's level, which was driven and graded in its own right.
- **Friction, at any intensity** (`--ux`, `--as-user`) — findings, never a level. A functioning-but-confusing unit is `works` plus findings. The one place friction meets the partition is when it is total, and then it is not friction: a framed step the user cannot complete at all went unobserved or exposed a real fault, and the unit lands on (c) or (b) accordingly.

`(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — the six degrade cells above, and the three-axis orthogonality (plus the composition order for the shared axis) that lets the partition skip the flag matrix. Because these are the runs that most plausibly land in no branch at all, and three of them are load-bearing: an unprovisionable sandbox must land `blocked` rather than quietly running against real state; an unexercisable persona pass must take a level rather than disappearing while the base unit's `works` reads as "works for everyone"; and the substitution cell is deliberately generalized past `--sandbox`, since an *ambient* stand-in under a claimed effect earns `works` over the same missing evidence as an isolated one.)`

## What this scale does not grade

It grades one thing: whether the unit behaved as claimed when driven. It does not grade how well the change is written, whether the automated suite covers it, why a fault happens, or how urgent a fault is — a `defective` unit carries no severity and no priority, and the caller who receives it decides those. Usability friction in particular sits on its own axis: recorded alongside the level, hunted deliberately under `--ux` and `--as-user`, and never by itself enough to move a functioning unit off `works`. The level tracks completion; the findings track cost of completion, and a unit can be maximally painful to complete and still be a unit that completed.
