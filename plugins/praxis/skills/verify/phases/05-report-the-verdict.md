This is the deliverable. Everything before it produced evidence; here the run says what it established, about which units, in which environment — and hands the work onward instead of doing it. Two failure modes pull in opposite directions and this phase is written against both: a verdict that claims more than the drives support (*"it works"*, from four flows out of thirty), and a finding nobody can act on without standing the application up again to find out what actually happened.

## Assign every graded unit exactly one level

Assign each unit **one of the four levels defined in [verdict-scale](../rules/verdict-scale.md)**, by working that rule's ordered questions in their order and taking the first stop they produce. The level names carry none of what makes this repeatable — the ordered questions, the adjacent-level discriminators, the tie-break direction, and the definition of the graded unit itself all live in that file, and an assignment made from the names alone is the divergence between two cold runs that the scale exists to close.

The inputs are already prepared. Phase 3's per-flow observation label answers the **coverage half** of the scale's observation question — *not observed* is "nothing was exercised", *fully observed* is "every framed step produced an observation entry" — while the **confirmation half**, whether each of those entries confirmed its step's claimed effect at its own layer, is read off the per-step entries and not off the label; and phase 4's per-failure attribution is what the fault question maps from. Close as they are, a label is not a level: run the scale's questions against them rather than translating labels into levels by eye, since the interesting units are exactly the ones where the mapping is not one-to-one (a *fully observed* flow whose third step misbehaved, a *partly observed* flow that also carries a reproduced defect, a *not observed* flow whose unreachability **is** the fault). Every level is reported with the scope it holds within, in the form the scale requires, and with its name unparaphrased.

Where the framed unit set came back **empty**, there is nothing for a level to attach to: the run resolves instead to one of the **two run-level terminal outcomes** the scale defines for an empty set, chosen by the guards it sets there — the confirmed absence of user-reachable behaviour under both guards, the unestablished framing where guard 1 fails. Check the guards rather than assuming the empty set qualifies, and report the outcome with no per-unit row and no level, since there is no unit.

## Roll the levels up into a headline that claims no more than the drives support

The run owes one headline above the per-unit levels, and it is computed, not composed. Resolve in order: **any unit `defective` → the headline is `defective`**; else **any unit `blocked` → `blocked`**; else **any unit `indeterminate` → `indeterminate`**; else **every unit `works` → `works`**. An empty unit set skips the ladder entirely: check the scale's guards first, then report the run-level terminal outcome they select — the confirmed-absence outcome or the unestablished-framing one — as the headline in its own right. The direction is not a stylistic choice: one reproduced fault refutes the claim outright and stays refuted, while no quantity of clean drives can establish the rest — so the headline follows the worst unit, never the average, never the majority, and never the flow the run happened to care most about.

The headline then carries three things without exception, because each is a way it silently overstates itself: the **units it covers**, named; the **environment** they were driven in, from the phase-2 record plus every repair phase 4 recorded; and the **units not driven or not settled**, named as such. That last set is never empty by omission — where an explicit flow list replaced the framed set, everything else is unobserved by construction, and saying so is the whole difference between a scoped result and a claim about the application. A headline that says *the application works* is wrong even when every unit came back `works`; write it about the units. `(basis: ratified by the maintainer 2026-07-27, on the build's recommendation — worst-unit-dominates in the order defective → blocked → indeterminate → works, with the unit set, the environment, and the un-driven set mandatory on the headline. The dominance direction is derived from the asymmetry the scale pins and is not open. The open cut is the ordering between the two levels that assert nothing: `blocked` is proposed ahead of `indeterminate` because it names an obstacle someone can clear, where `indeterminate` names an observation still to complete, and a headline should surface the thing that stops the next run.)`

Report the tally alongside the headline — how many units at each level — so a `defective` headline over nineteen clean units and one over a single driven unit do not read identically.

## What a defect finding must carry to be actionable

The bar is that a reader who was not at the run can reproduce the failure without asking a question, and can start work without re-driving anything to find out what happened. Each finding carries: the unit and the step it broke at; the entry point and starting state it was driven from; the interaction sequence in order; **expected-versus-observed at the failing step, quoted from the drive record** rather than paraphrased; the layer the effect was checked at; the environment with its repairs; the attribution from phase 4 with the test that decided it, the drive ratio, and whether it was located by comparison or by reach; and, for a narrowed drive, what was not exercised. `(basis: the field list is fixed by what the handoff below consumes — every item is something the receiving skill would otherwise reconstruct by standing the application up again, which is the cost this phase exists to pay once, and the reproduction was already executed once as the re-drive in phase 4, so it is a tested artifact rather than a description.)`

What a finding must **not** carry is a mechanism. This run established that a fault is real and where it lies, not why it happens — so quote the observation and label every causal reading as an inference, by the discriminator in [observation-over-inference](../rules/observation-over-inference.md). A plausible cause written as fact sends the next reader down your guess instead of the evidence, which is worse than offering nothing.

Three neighbours report in the same shape and the same place, each labelled, so that none of them is either lost or mistaken for the change's own defect: an **environment fault**, labelled as one, so nobody re-hunts it as a code bug; a **pre-existing defect**, labelled so the reader knows this change is not answerable for it — it is still a genuine defect, and it does move its unit's level, because the scale's fault question maps that attribution to a fault answer and the level tracks whether the unit behaved as claimed while the tag carries answerability; and a failure attributed as **not established**, given a prominent line saying what was missing that would have settled it, so the reader knows the label was forced by absent evidence rather than earned by it.

## Usability findings, in a section of their own

Friction findings report in their own section, never merged into a functional level and never modifying one — [ux-walkthrough](../modules/ux-walkthrough.md) owns why the two may not collapse in either direction. They arrive already filtered, each recorded against that module's reportability bar at the moment it was observed, so this phase neither re-litigates that bar nor admits a finding at report time that was never recorded against it. `(basis: phase 3's record discipline, which is that a finding not written down against the bar when it was observed is a recollection; a report may relay the record, not upgrade it.)`

Friction findings are **not graded or ranked** here, and neither is the functional side — no severity, no priority, on a friction finding or on a `defective` unit alike ([verdict-scale](../rules/verdict-scale.md) fixes what the scale does not grade). Each finding carries its step and its anchor; prioritisation belongs to whoever owns the change.

Where flows were driven as a named persona, every finding from that pass is scoped to that persona's path and says so; a claim about users in general drawn from one persona's drive is an inference and is labelled one. **Where an experiential pass could not be performed at all**, report it as not performed with the reason, as narrowing — never silently absent, and never substituted with a reading of the code. That is distinct from a flow-and-persona *unit* whose access path could not be exercised: that unit is graded, by the scale's degrade cases, and does not disappear into a footnote while the base unit's `works` reads as *works for everyone*.

## Under `--from-spec` — report per requirement

The graded unit becomes the requirement, so the report is keyed to the spec's requirements rather than to flows: each requirement takes its level from the flow or flows that exercised it, rolled up over that subset by the same rule as the headline, and a flow that exercised several requirements reports under each. A requirement that **no flow could reach** is a unit that exists and was not driven — it takes the level the scale gives that case and is reported as **unobserved**, with the reason it could not be reached (nothing resolves to it, the behaviour is absent, it is unreachable in this environment). Never as met, never as failed, and above all never *missing from the report*: a requirement quietly absent from a per-requirement report reads as covered, which is the one failure mode this reporting mode exists to prevent.

The converse case arises only with `--flows` set as well, and it reports rather than vanishing: a **driven flow that exercises no requirement** takes its level like any unit and appears in a short section of its own, keyed by flow name and labelled as outside the spec's requirements, so it neither disappears from a per-requirement report nor gets filed under a requirement it did not exercise. It counts in the tally and in the headline ladder, because it is a unit the run graded. `(basis: the same rule this mode exists for — anything quietly absent from a keyed report reads as covered, and the caller who listed the flow is owed its level; keying it by flow rather than by requirement is what keeps the requirement column honest.)`

A partially exercised requirement splits rather than rounding: the reached part takes its level, the unreached part is named as unobserved, and the requirement is not reported as met on the strength of the half that ran. And the headline in this mode may not read as *the spec is satisfied* while any requirement is unobserved — the honest form states how many requirements were established, how many failed, and how many were never reached.

## Hand off; do not fix

verify's deliverable is the verdict, and it repairs nothing: a run that turns its own finding green has verified a change nobody else has seen. Each finding leaves by a named invocation, not by work performed here.

- A defect whose **mechanism is not established** — the common case, since phase 4 stops at attribution — goes to **debug**: invoke [debug](../../debug/SKILL.md) with the reproduction as its seed, and take its diagnosis as the return. Do not root-cause it here.
- A defect whose mechanism **is** established and whose fix is understood goes to **develop**: invoke [develop](../../develop/SKILL.md) with the finding as the work item. The discriminator between these two is whether the mechanism is established — not how small the fix looks, which is exactly the judgment a run that has not diagnosed anything is worst placed to make.
- Getting the verdict **in front of people** goes to **communicate**: invoke [communicate](../../communicate/SKILL.md) with the verdict as its input. verify routes nothing itself and deliberately has no flag for it.
- An **environment fault** goes back to whoever owns that environment, with the repair — or the reason none was attempted — recorded. It is not a code change and does not become develop's work item.

**When invoking a sibling skill is not available in this context** — a lone executor with no way to hand anything off — the report names the invocation that should be made against each specific finding, and stops there. Naming the handoff discharges the obligation; doing the other skill's work in its place does not.

## Output

The verdict, returned to the caller: the headline with its unit set, its environment, and its un-driven set — or, for an empty unit set, the run-level terminal outcome in its place; the per-unit levels with their scope, or per-requirement status under `--from-spec` with unobserved requirements called out and any driven-but-unmapped flow in its own section; a reproduction-bearing finding for every defect, with environment faults, pre-existing defects, and not-established failures labelled as such; usability findings in their own section; and the named handoff for each finding.
