# Structure for scanning

Almost no one reads a work artifact top to bottom; they scan for the part that concerns them, and if they can't find it in a few seconds they either read nothing or read the wrong thing. Structure is what makes an artifact navigable at a glance — and it's a different craft from ordering the content (that's [lead-with-the-takeaway](lead-with-the-takeaway.md)): ordering decides what comes first, structure decides how a skimmer *finds their part* without reading in order. It is applied while laying the skeleton in [draft-the-content](../phases/05-draft-the-content.md).

## Make the skeleton legible without reading the prose

Build the artifact so its shape is readable before its words are:

- **Headings that name content, not sections** — "Rollback plan," not "Section 3"; "What changes for callers," not "Details." A skimmer reads only the headings first; each must tell them whether their part is inside.
- **Lists for anything enumerable** — steps, options, requirements, findings. A wall of prose hides its items; a list exposes them and lets the reader count and locate.
- **Bolded anchors** on the load-bearing phrase in a dense paragraph, so the eye lands on the point even mid-block. One or two per paragraph — bold everything and nothing stands out.
- **One idea per paragraph**, short enough to skip or absorb as a unit; a paragraph that turns needs to become two.

## The scanning test

The test that says the structure works: **can a reader who reads only the headings, the bolded anchors, and the first line of each paragraph reconstruct the takeaway and find where their concern is addressed?** If the point only emerges from reading full paragraphs, the structure is failing the skimmer — surface it into a heading or an anchor. `(basis: NN/g eye-tracking research on how users read online — scanning in an F-pattern, reading headings and the starts of lines — and plain-language layout guidance; the anchors/headings/lists move set is the well-corroborated response to it.)`

## Match structure to form

Structure scales to the form chosen in [choose-form-and-channel](../phases/03-choose-form-and-channel.md): a durable doc earns full headings and a scannable hierarchy; a short message earns a lead line and maybe a short list, not a heading tree that dwarfs its content. Imposing document scaffolding on a three-sentence message is its own scanning failure — the structure should be proportionate to what's being structured.
