# Meet the learner where they are

Teaching is a distinct mode from informing, and it fails in a distinct way: the writer explains from where *they* stand — the finished mental model, all the pieces in place — and the learner, who has only some of the pieces, can't attach the new one to anything. Onboarding notes, mentoring messages, and walkthroughs that read as reference material leave the learner able to *follow* the explanation but not to *do the thing next time*. This rule is the learning-mode overlay: it layers on top of whichever audience tier the reader occupies (a peer being onboarded is a peer *in learning mode*), and it applies in [draft-the-content](../phases/04-draft-the-content.md) when [frame-the-message](../phases/01-frame-the-message.md) flagged the mode.

## Build from the known toward the gap

Start from what the learner already holds and bridge to the new thing, rather than laying out the target model and expecting them to climb into it:

- **Anchor on their existing knowledge** — connect the new concept to something they already understand ("this works like the retry logic you already know, except…"), so the new piece attaches to an existing one.
- **Order by dependency, not by conclusion** — this is the one place [lead-with-the-takeaway](lead-with-the-takeaway.md) yields: a learner sometimes needs the derivation *before* the conclusion, so they can follow how you got there and reproduce it. State where you're headed, then build to it — but deliberately, as a teaching choice, not as buried-lede sloppiness.
- **Make implicit steps explicit** — the steps an expert skips are exactly the ones the learner is missing; spell out what "obviously" you'd do.

## Explain the why, so they can generalize

The difference between teaching and dictating: a learner given only the steps can repeat *this* case and is stuck on the next one; a learner given the *reasoning* can generalize to cases you didn't cover. Explain why each step is what it is, so the learner builds a model they can extend — the goal is that they don't need you next time, not that they can copy this once. `(basis: worked-example and minimalist-instruction pedagogy — Carroll — plus the Diátaxis distinction between acquisition (learning) and application (doing): a learner is acquiring a transferable skill, so the reasoning that enables transfer is the content, not decoration.)`

## Prefer the worked example, and keep it safe-to-fail

For a learner, a worked example is often the primary teaching instrument, not a supplement ([show-dont-just-tell](show-dont-just-tell.md)) — walk one real case end to end. And frame it safe-to-fail: name the mistakes that are easy to make and cheap to recover, so the learner explores rather than freezes for fear of breaking something. The tone is patient and never condescending ([calibrate-tone-to-context](calibrate-tone-to-context.md)): more scaffolding is meeting them where they are, not treating them as less capable.
