# Clean export

Everything communicate hands to a human is an export of the work's *substance* for that audience — the content and the decisions, never the machinery that produced them. The reader wants the decision, not the fact that an explorer surfaced it; the status, not that a critic flagged it; the recommendation, not which phase composed it. Internal-process references leak the producer's world into the reader's, force them to learn machinery irrelevant to them, and — for an external reader — expose how the sausage is made where only the sausage was asked for. This is a ratified house standard, not a style preference. It is applied in [draft-the-content](../phases/05-draft-the-content.md) (draft clean) and enforced as a check in [tighten-and-verify](../phases/06-tighten-and-verify.md), and it re-applies before any publish in [deliver-and-route](../phases/07-deliver-and-route.md).

`(basis: ratified house decision — "artifacts are team-facing documents": anything written for a human audience carries the content and the decisions and none of the internal machinery. The producing skill strips every internal-process reference before handoff; the port publishes faithfully and adds no process metadata of its own. This is the same bar operate applies to a published incident record.)`

## What counts as an internal-process reference

Strip all of these — they are the machinery, not the substance:

- **Tool and backend names** — the concrete tools, providers, or services used to produce or deliver the artifact.
- **Agent / phase / skill / critic mechanics** — "the code explorer found," "in the triage phase," "the user-advocate flagged," "this skill's draft step," or any reference to the procedure's internal structure.
- **praxis process** — that the work went through praxis at all, which skill ran, or how the pipeline is organized.
- **Provenance of production** — "this was generated by," "after fanning out to sub-agents," "an automated pass produced this," run logs, phase traces, or a "how this was made" note.

## The discriminator — machinery vs content

The test that separates a leak from legitimate content, applied sentence by sentence: **removing the sentence loses *how the artifact was produced* but no fact the reader acts on → it is machinery, strip it. Removing it loses a fact the reader needs → it is content, keep it.**

The subtlety is that some process *is* the content: a decision record legitimately describes the *engineering* process it documents ("we ran a two-week benchmark") — that is the substance the reader acts on. What must go is the *praxis* process that produced the artifact ("the benchmark data was gathered by the code explorer in the draft phase"). Ask: is this process a fact about *the work*, or a fact about *how I wrote this up*? The first stays; the second goes.

**The declared-absence case, which the same test resolves.** An artifact may legitimately state that something it owed the reader is not established ([source-or-declare](source-or-declare.md) sets when that is owed and what it must say). Such a sentence is **content** — it tells the reader where the answer lives and what would settle it, which are facts they act on. What it may not carry is the *cause* of a failed lookup: an unconfigured or unavailable backend, an errored read. Those are production history and this rule strips them. The two rules agree here by construction rather than by precedence — the declaration is written at the reader's altitude and never states the run's internal condition, so nothing in it reaches the strip list.

**When the work being documented IS a praxis artifact, its names are the subject — keep them.** A decision record *about* a praxis skill, port, or config key (this repo documents its own tooling constantly) legitimately names `communicate`, the `communication` port, `tools.communication` — those are the *subject the reader must act on*, not machinery to strip. The strip-list targets how *this write-up* was produced, never the names of the praxis things the decision is *about*. The test is unchanged — a fact the reader acts on stays — but do not let the bright-line strip-list genericize a record into uselessness ("the messaging capability," "the skill in question") when the precise names are exactly what makes it actionable. Strip the production trace; keep the subject, even when the subject is praxis itself.

## Anchors

- *Leaked (the defect):* "Based on the repository explorer's scan and the completeness-auditor's review, this decision record captures the caching choice." → Names the machinery; the reader neither needs nor should see it.
- *Clean (the fix):* "This records the decision to adopt the hybrid cache and why." → The substance, nothing about how it was assembled.
- *Legitimate process (keep):* "We benchmarked three strategies over two weeks before deciding." → This is a fact about the engineering work the reader is evaluating, not about how the write-up was produced.
- *Praxis-as-subject (keep the names, strip the trace):* "**Decision: shed `tools.communication` from the `communicate` skill; route messaging through the existing `communication` port.**" → The skill, port, and config names are the subject the reader acts on — keep them. What would still be stripped: "the standards-skeptic flagged this during the gate phase." Over-stripping the subject to "the messaging capability" would make the record unactionable.

## The split is owned on both sides

communicate strips every internal-process reference *before* handing the artifact to a port; the port ([communication](../../communication/SKILL.md) or [publish-artifact](../../publish-artifact/SKILL.md)) publishes or posts it faithfully and adds no process metadata of its own — no "authored via praxis" footer, no branding, no trace. Neither half is optional: a clean producer with a port that stamped a footer would leak, and a faithful port fed a dirty draft would publish the leak. communicate owns its half — the artifact leaves this skill clean.
