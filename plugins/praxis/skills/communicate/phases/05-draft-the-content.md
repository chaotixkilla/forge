The frame, the tier, the form, and the sourced requirement set from [derive-and-source](04-derive-and-source.md) are fixed inputs, and each drafting judgment routes to the rule that pins it. The sections below are the drafting order: skeleton first, then fill, then the craft passes that make it read for *this* reader.

**Draft from the requirement set, not from the session.** Every entry goes in — an obtained requirement as text, an unobtained one as the declaration [source-or-declare](../../../craft/writing/source-or-declare.md) pins for its disposition. Reaching past the set for something that merely felt relevant is how unsourced material re-enters after the phase that existed to keep it out.

## Lay the skeleton: takeaway first, structured to scan

Open with the takeaway fixed in [frame-the-message](01-frame-the-message.md) — the conclusion or ask in the first sentence or paragraph, so a reader who stops there still knows what to do. The method for putting the point first (and telling it apart from the setup that wants to come first) is [lead-with-the-takeaway](../../../craft/writing/lead-with-the-takeaway.md). Then lay headings, lists, and bolded anchors so a skimming reader finds their part without reading top to bottom — [structure-for-scanning](../../../craft/writing/structure-for-scanning.md) carries the layout method. The skeleton exists before the prose because a reader scans structure before they read words.

## Fill to the tier's prescription

Write the body to the prescription the tier carries in [audience-tiers](../../../craft/writing/audience-tiers.md) — depth, assumed knowledge, and framing are set by the tier assigned in [model-the-audience](02-model-the-audience.md), not chosen afresh here. **When the assigned tier is a split** (a depth-tier + a framing-tier, e.g. "peer-depth / exec-framing" for a high-proximity decision-maker), take depth, jargon, and assumed knowledge from the depth-tier and framing, lead, and what-to-minimize from the framing-tier — do not apply either tier's whole prescription. Two craft rules refine the fill:

- **Vocabulary** — use the terms the reader already uses; define or drop the jargon they don't share rather than displaying it. [match-reader-vocabulary](../../../craft/writing/match-reader-vocabulary.md) pins when a term is defined-on-first-use, dropped, or used bare, keyed to the tier.
- **Detail altitude** — give exactly the detail the decision or action needs; defer the rest to an appendix or a link. [right-size-the-detail](../../../craft/writing/right-size-the-detail.md) carries the need-to-know test that sorts every candidate detail into inline / appendix / link, and the stopping test that says when to stop cutting or adding.

## Ground the abstract, and preserve the why

Three content rules apply where the type calls for them:

- Where the artifact makes an abstract claim the reader must grasp or trust, ground it in one concrete example, before/after, or sample — [show-dont-just-tell](../../../craft/writing/show-dont-just-tell.md) pins when an example is owed versus when it is padding.
- Where the artifact is an **incident retrospective**, it owes what [incident-retrospective](../rules/incident-retrospective.md) lists: the timeline, the contributing factors, the blameless framing and the follow-ups.
- Where the artifact is a **decision record** (or any type that will be re-litigated), record the reasoning and the rejected alternatives, not just the conclusion. [preserve-the-why](../../../craft/writing/preserve-the-why.md) carries what a preserved why must contain.

## Place the visuals the requirement set already assigned

Which requirements became a table, a diagram, or a chart was decided in [derive-and-source](04-derive-and-source.md) against the content's shape; drafting places them, it does not re-open the choice. Put each where the reader meets the requirement it discharges, not in a figures section at the end. Render diagrams to the notation, size, edge-labeling, and declared-elision bars in [diagram-legibility](../../../craft/writing/diagram-legibility.md). Two things come from [when-a-visual-is-owed](../../../craft/writing/when-a-visual-is-owed.md) at placement time: the **one-home rule** — where a visual carries a requirement, the prose points at it rather than saying it again — and the **chart's form**, which that rule sets by where the content is read: a chart block in a published document, the figures table elsewhere.

## Pitch the voice, and scaffold for a learner

Calibrate register and tone per [calibrate-tone-to-context](../../../craft/writing/calibrate-tone-to-context.md), starting from the destination's voice read in [model-the-audience](02-model-the-audience.md). When the learning-mode overlay is set, layer [meet-the-learner-where-they-are](../../../craft/writing/meet-the-learner-where-they-are.md) on top.

## Make the ask land, and keep the export clean

State the ask (or its explicit absence) where the reader will act on it, per [make-the-ask-explicit](../../../craft/writing/make-the-ask-explicit.md) — usually near the takeaway, not stranded at the end. And draft the artifact as a **clean export from the start**, carrying none of the machinery that produced it: [clean-export](../../../craft/writing/clean-export.md) defines what an internal-process reference is and how to tell it from legitimate content. Drafting clean is cheaper than scrubbing later, and [tighten-and-verify](06-tighten-and-verify.md) checks it.

## Produce in the target language under `--lang`

The base draft is written in the work's default language. Under `--lang=<code>`, produce it in the target language instead — see [localize-and-translate](../modules/localize-and-translate.md).

Done-state: a complete draft exists — takeaway-first, scannable, pitched to the tier's depth and vocabulary, grounded where it claims, carrying the why where the type owes it, in the right register and language, with an explicit ask and no internal-process references. It is ready to tighten, not yet delivered.
