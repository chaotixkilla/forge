# parallel-fan-out (`--max-agents=<n>`)

Activated by `--max-agents=<n>`, referenced from SKILL.md as a whole-run module (it reshapes [pick-the-cheapest-probe](../phases/03-pick-the-cheapest-probe.md) through [evaluate-against-the-question](../phases/05-evaluate-against-the-question.md)).

The base spike runs one probe against the question. This module runs *up to n candidate approaches to the same question in parallel* and picks between them on evidence — the empirical bake-off for when two or more approaches look plausible and argument won't separate them. **Deletion test:** remove it and prototype runs a single probe and returns its verdict; the racing, the comparison, and the selection are the added, flag-gated behavior.

## The delta

- **Pick N genuinely different approaches** (in [pick-the-cheapest-probe](../phases/03-pick-the-cheapest-probe.md)) — where **genuinely different** means a *distinct mechanism or strategy* that answers the question a different way (a different library, algorithm, data model, or architecture), **not the same approach re-parameterized**. A parameter sweep — the same approach across batch sizes 100/1k/10k — is **one** approach, not N; if the question *is* the parameter curve, that's a single probe measuring across values in [build-the-spike](../phases/04-build-the-spike.md), not a race. Each candidate still obeys the cheapest-probe basis (each must exercise the real risk, or its verdict is uninformative). `(basis: ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-09. "Distinct mechanism, not re-parameterization" bounds what --max-agents races so two cold runs assemble the same *kind* of candidate set — without it, one executor races technologies and another races tunings of one technology, resolving different sub-questions and selecting different kinds of winner. No external authority pins this axis of difference, so it is the maintainer's ratified house standard.)`
- **Build and run each** (in [build-the-spike](../phases/04-build-the-spike.md), [evaluate-against-the-question](../phases/05-evaluate-against-the-question.md)) — each gets its own [verdict-scale](../rules/verdict-scale.md) verdict and evidence, isolated so one approach's failure doesn't muddy another's signal.
- **Compare and select** (in [evaluate-against-the-question](../phases/05-evaluate-against-the-question.md)) — on the basis below.

## Thin module — defers the mechanics

`(basis: ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-09 — thin module, defers mechanics.)` This module owns only the prototype-specific semantics: *what* to race (candidate approaches to one question), and *how to compare and select* them. The orchestration mechanics — fanning out the workers, honoring the `n` cap, collecting results — are the harness's, reached through the same parallel-orchestration (fan-out) capability the explorer scout already uses; this module does not re-implement them. Naming the capability, not the plumbing, is what keeps prototype from duplicating orchestration that lives once at the harness level.

## The comparison basis

Compare the raced approaches in two stages, and use the *method* the second stage names — the method is where bias creeps in:

1. **Verdict first (a screen).** An approach that reached **answered** (it works) outranks one still at **still-open** (unknown) outranks one **refuted** (it doesn't work). Resolving the unknown is the point, so a cheaper approach that only reached *still-open* loses to a costlier one that reached *answered* — saving effort never beats answering the question. Approaches that reached *refuted* are out.
2. **Among same-verdict approaches, compare relative to a datum — not by a weighted score.** Fix the axes *before* looking at any result (so the criteria aren't reverse-engineered to justify a favorite): **effort-to-production** (real work remaining to ship this approach), **remaining unknowns** (what each leaves unresolved or newly raises), and **fit to the constraints the question named** ([frame-the-question](../phases/01-frame-the-question.md)). Pick a **strong datum** — the incumbent approach, or the simplest candidate — and rate every other approach on each axis as *better / worse / same* **relative to that datum**, not on an absolute 1–5 scale. Prefer the approach that best serves the decision *and* leaves the fewest load-bearing unknowns. When approaches come out close, **treat the near-tie as a tie**: do not manufacture a winner from a fragile gap — break it by *reversibility* (prefer the approach cheapest to undo if it proves wrong). Guard against the standing biases: don't anchor on the first or the most *polished* candidate — polish is an illusion of completeness, not evidence it's better.

`(basis: ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-09. The *method* — ordinal, relative to a strong datum, near-ties-as-ties, criteria fixed before viewing — is authoritatively preferred over one-shot weighted numeric scoring: Pugh controlled convergence (score +/−/S against a datum, iterate) and Frey et al. 2009 (relative-to-datum + iteration beats weighted-sum/Borda, tied to Arrow's impossibility theorem — there is no bias-free way to collapse several criteria into one number). ATAM informs the *axis* idea (compare on the quality attributes the decision turns on) but deliberately surfaces risks/tradeoffs rather than picking a winner. The specific three-axis set and the reversibility tie-break are house-selected, so the whole basis is the maintainer's ratified house standard. "Prototype bake-off" has no authoritative definition — folklore — so it is not used as a method.)`

To run stage 2, recruit the **trade-off-analyst** critic ([trade-off-analyst](../../../agents/critics/trade-off-analyst.md)) — its lens reconstructs, for each pair of surviving approaches, the two-sided cost (what each avoids that the other pays), which is exactly the relative-to-datum comparison above made explicit. Grade its findings on this module's basis, not one it brings (prototype declares no severity scale, so it states consequence and the size of the gap in plain terms — the critic's own contract). **Without fan-out**, apply the trade-off-analyst's lens yourself: pick the datum, then for each other approach write the one-line better/worse/same on each fixed axis before selecting — a selection with only one side priced is not made. Record the comparison as lightweight pros/cons against the datum (a decision-record shape), not a scored matrix.

The output: the selected approach, its verdict and evidence, and the priced comparison against the runners-up (the rejected approaches are themselves durable learnings — [record-dead-ends](../rules/record-dead-ends.md)).
