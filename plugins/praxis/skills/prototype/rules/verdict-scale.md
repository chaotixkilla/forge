# The verdict scale

The verdict answers one question: **did something actually run and observe the framed unknown, and which way did it point?** It takes one result from [results-and-certainty](../../../craft/evidence/results-and-certainty.md) — `holds`, `fails`, `unsettled` or `not checked` — and that standard owns the words, what they mean, and the asymmetry between `holds` and `fails`; this rule owns how a spike places its run among them. It rests entirely on observed behavior, never on reasoning about the code ([observation-over-inference](../../../craft/evidence/observation-over-inference.md)); how far the result generalizes beyond what ran is a separate axis ([keep-the-real-thing-in-view](keep-the-real-thing-in-view.md)). It is assigned in [evaluate-against-the-question](../phases/05-evaluate-against-the-question.md), consumed by [capture-and-discard](../phases/06-capture-and-discard.md) (it leads the findings blob) and by the [parallel-fan-out](../modules/parallel-fan-out.md) module (raced approaches are compared verdict-first).

Under the standard's asymmetry, the strongest a `holds` can be here is *exercised under the spike's conditions and not refuted*. Word and read the verdict accordingly.

## The four results

`(basis: maintainer, 2026-07-09; after Popper via the SEP, Fisher 1935 and the winner/loser/inconclusive split experimentation platforms report; the fourth, not checked, maintainer, 2026-10-02)`

- **holds** — the probe ran, exercised the framed unknown itself, and produced a stable observation pointing *toward* feasibility: the assumption holds under the conditions tested, and the decision the question unblocks can be made on that evidence.
  - *Anchor (top of scale):* the question was "can library X sustain 10k writes/sec on our shape of data?" The spike ran X against production-shaped data and held ~12k/sec across three runs. **holds** — the throughput assumption is met, with margin.
- **fails** — the probe ran, exercised the framed unknown, and produced a stable observation demonstrating it *fails*: the assumption is falsified, the approach is off the table for this question.
  - *Anchor:* the same question — X sustained only ~1.2k/sec and GC-stalled under the real payload, repeatably. **fails** — the throughput assumption is refuted; X is off the table.
- **unsettled** — the probe ran and exercised the framed unknown, but the observation does not conclusively resolve the question, in *either* direction: the conditions fell short of what the question named while still letting the risk show, the signal is partial or ambiguous, the result did not reproduce, or the spike simply *did not show the assumption failing* without positively demonstrating it works. It is an honest, expected result — the common outcome, not a failure of the spike — and it is what a timebox expiry reports when something ran and the answer wasn't reached.
  - *Anchor:* the throughput spike ran real serialization on production-shaped data, but its three runs came back at 9k, 14k and 11k/sec. **unsettled** — the real risk was exercised and the observation doesn't hold still long enough to read.
- **not checked** — the framed unknown itself was never exercised: the probe stubbed the very thing in doubt, ran at a data shape or scale where the risk can't show at all (a race on a single thread, a contention cost on one record), or nothing ran before the timebox expired. The reason is always stated.
  - *Anchor (bottom of scale):* the throughput spike hit 15k/sec — but it stubbed the serialization step, the very thing the question turned on. **not checked**, because serialization was stubbed — the promising number says nothing about the framed unknown.

## Judge against the pre-committed success test

Assign the verdict against the observation named *before building* in [frame-the-question](../phases/01-frame-the-question.md) — not a bar chosen after seeing the result. Moving the goalpost to fit what the spike happened to produce (lowering the throughput target, slicing to the subset that passed, re-running until a green appears) manufactures a false `holds`. `(basis: Thoughtworks, "How to Implement Hypothesis-Driven Development"; the experimentation literature on post-hoc adjudication and peeking)`

## The adjacent-result discriminators

- **holds vs fails** — *direction of the conclusive observation.* Both require the identical evidentiary bar (the real unknown exercised, stably observed); they differ only in sign, and in weight, by the asymmetry above — never round a `holds` up to a proof.
- **fails vs unsettled** — *demonstrated failure vs absence of success.* `fails` needs the run to positively *show the assumption failing* (it ran the real thing and it didn't work). A spike that merely *didn't manage to show it working* — ran out of time with the real component running, produced an ambiguous result — is **unsettled**, not `fails`. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence; collapsing "I didn't see it work" into "it doesn't work" is the most common way an unsettled run is mis-reported, and it wrongly kills viable approaches. `(basis: Lakens on the absence-of-evidence fallacy)`
- **holds vs unsettled** — *this is the load-bearing boundary, where "how much evidence is enough" is decided.* A run **holds** only when **both** hold: **(1)** the run exercised the framed unknown *itself* — the actual risky component, under conditions faithful to what the question named (real-enough data, scale, integration), not a stub, mock, or toy substitute *of the very thing in doubt* ([change-one-thing-at-a-time](../../../craft/evidence/change-one-thing-at-a-time.md), [keep-the-real-thing-in-view](keep-the-real-thing-in-view.md)); **and (2)** the observation is *stable* — it reproduced, not a single lucky or unlucky run. If **either** is missing, the run does not hold, no matter how promising the number looked: it is `unsettled`, or `not checked` where the next discriminator says so. "Enough" is not a count of runs; it is: *the framed unknown was observed under conditions faithful to the question, and the observation held.*
- **unsettled vs not checked** — *was the framed unknown exercised at all?* The real thing in doubt ran, under conditions where its risk can show → `unsettled` (or better, by the tests above). It was stubbed or substituted, the conditions could not let the risk show at all, or nothing ran → `not checked`, with that reason. A probe that ran plenty of code, none of it the thing in doubt, is `not checked`. `(basis: maintainer, 2026-10-02)`

When `holds` and `unsettled` both seem to fit, `unsettled` wins unless you can name the observation that exercised the real risk and state that it reproduced. Overstating an unsettled run as `holds` ships a decision resting on evidence that was never gathered. Cross-check any `holds` against [observation-over-inference](../../../craft/evidence/observation-over-inference.md): if the claim rests on what the code *should* do rather than what a run *showed*, it does not hold.
