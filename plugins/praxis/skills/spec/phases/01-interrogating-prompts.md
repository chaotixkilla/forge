The prompt is where a spec is won or lost. A request arrives compressed — "add sharing," "make it faster," "let users export their data" — and the compression hides the scope: the actors it implies, the states it never mentions, the defaults the author assumed you already share. This phase attacks the request *before* a single requirement is written, expanding the compressed ask into the full space of what it actually entails. Skip it and you spec the sentence instead of the need — the most common way a spec is confidently wrong.

When `--from-issue` or `--from-discussion` is set, the request is seeded from an external source rather than typed inline ([ingest-from-issue](../modules/ingest-from-issue.md), [ingest-from-discussion](../modules/ingest-from-discussion.md)) — but a seeded request is interrogated exactly as hard as a hand-entered one; the source changes, the scrutiny does not.

## Decompose the nouns and verbs

Run a mechanical pass: nouns → **entities** (things the system knows about), verbs → **actions** (things done to them). "Users share documents with teams" yields entities (user, document, team) and an action (share); this pass alone surfaces most of the scope for free. The discriminator between an entity and an attribute decides how much a thing costs to build, so pin it here: **does it have its own identity and lifecycle (entity), or is it a property of something else (attribute)?** A "share" that can be revoked, expire, and be audited is an entity; a `shared` boolean flag is an attribute — and *which one the request means* is itself a requirement to resolve, not a detail to assume.

## Find the actors

List every actor the request implies — human roles (admin, end user, guest) and non-human ones (a scheduled job, a third-party webhook, the system itself acting on a timer). Each actor earns its place by implying its own flows and permissions. The discriminator for whether two roles are one actor or two: **do they differ in permissions, flow, or goal?** If an "admin" and an "editor" can do exactly the same things in this feature, they are one actor; the moment one can do something the other cannot, they are two — and that difference is a requirement. This actor list is the spine every requirement later traces back to ([trace-each-requirement-to-a-need](../rules/trace-each-requirement-to-a-need.md)).

## Hunt the implicit requirements

The dangerous requirements are the ones the prompt silently assumed: file-size limits, what happens on failure, where data lives, when it is cleaned up, who can see what, and **any legal, regulatory, or compliance regime the feature falls under** (a data-portability or privacy law, a retention mandate, an accessibility obligation, an audit requirement). That last one is easy to skip and often the highest-leverage unsaid, because it silently governs format, timeline, and completeness — the same fuzzy ask under a compliance obligation versus as a product convenience yields specs of entirely different character, so name the regime (or its absence) rather than defaulting to "convenience" by omission. Sweep for the defaults the author had in mind but never wrote, and record each as an assumption or an open question ([make-the-unsaid-explicit](../rules/make-the-unsaid-explicit.md)) rather than absorbing it silently. The test for whether you have found one: **can you name something the request would obviously need but never mentioned?** If you can, it is implicit, and it goes on the page where it can be challenged — not into your head where it becomes a late surprise.

## Ask the five clarifiers

For each capability the request implies, drive it to specifics with five questions: **what triggers it? what is the happy path? what are the edge cases? what does success look like? what does failure look like?** The last two are where testability begins — a concretely stated "success" and "failure" are the seeds of the acceptance criteria written in [making-it-concrete](04-making-it-concrete.md). Keep every answer at the level of *what and why*, never *how*: a clarifier that starts answering "how would we build it" has crossed into design, which is plan's to decide, not spec's ([separate-problem-from-solution](../rules/separate-problem-from-solution.md)).

## Gather the domain grounding, and attack your own assumptions

Two moves close the phase. **First, gather the grounding** — delegate to the `gather` skill ([gather](../../gather/SKILL.md)), which pulls the glossary and domain terms (what these words mean *here*, not in general), known actor types, and whether the entities you are inventing already exist in the codebase — recruiting the knowledge-base, code, and community-practices lanes and returning one weighted picture. spec does not read those backends itself; `gather` owns that read — reaching the knowledge backend through the [knowledge](../../knowledge/SKILL.md) port — and owns its degrade (if the knowledge backend is unset, `gather` says so and returns what it can). Without the `gather` delegation available, read whatever domain context you can reach inline before proceeding — the grounding is not optional, only the delegation is. When *nothing* is reachable (a bare request, no repo or glossary in scope), proceed on the request as given but record the absent grounding as a global assumption ([make-the-unsaid-explicit](../rules/make-the-unsaid-explicit.md)), so the whole spec is read as assumption-laden rather than falsely grounded.

**Second, attack the request** — recruit the **assumption-hunter** critic ([assumption-hunter](../../../agents/critics/assumption-hunter.md)), whose lens is "what is this asking for that no one has said out loud?", and fold its surfaced assumptions into the list. This is spec's adversarial edge, applied to the interrogation. Without fan-out, run the lens yourself: walk the request a second time in a deliberately skeptical pass, naming every default you are silently taking, before you move on.

The output is the expanded request — entities, actions, actors, and a list of surfaced assumptions and open questions — the raw material [pin-down-ambiguity](02-pin-down-ambiguity.md) hardens into constraints.
