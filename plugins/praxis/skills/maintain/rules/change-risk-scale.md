# The change-risk scale

Every maintenance change carries a risk — how far its effects reach, and how hard it is to undo — and that risk decides *how* the change is allowed to land: changed directly, staged behind a guard, or given a migration path. Leave the risk a prose "use judgment" and the same change gets made three different ways: one maintainer edits a shared format in place, another stages it, a third breaks a downstream consumer who was never asked. This rule pins the scale so two cold executors grade a borderline change onto the same tier and take the same action. It is maintain's severity-scale analog: the one graded judgment the whole procedure turns on.

Assigned in [understand-blast-radius](../phases/02-understand-blast-radius.md), and consumed in [make-the-change](../phases/03-make-the-change.md) as the mandatory action each tier forces.

## Grade on two axes, take the worse

A change's tier is the **worse (higher) of two independent axes** — evaluate them highest-first (test *exposed*, then *bounded*, else *contained*):

- **Reach** — how far outside the edited unit the change is observed: nothing outside the unit → an enumerable set of consumers you can move → an external, non-enumerable, or cross-boundary set you cannot.
- **Reversibility** — how cheaply it backs out: a single clean revert → revertible only via a runtime guard (it is live the instant it lands) → irreversible because data or state is already written.

Taking the max is what makes the three tiers **partition** — every maintenance change lands in exactly one, and *either* axis alone can force the escalation. A migration that writes data into a private, in-unit table is `exposed` on reversibility even though its reach is local; a rename with no revert difficulty but many external importers is `exposed` on reach even though backout is a clean revert.

**Test coverage is not a third axis.** Whether the change is covered by tests changes how confidently you can *verify* it ([verify-and-guard](../phases/04-verify-and-guard.md)), not how far it reaches or how it reverts. An untested but behavior-preserving local change is still `contained`; its missing coverage is a gap surfaced to the [test](../../test/SKILL.md) skill, never a reason to bump the tier.

**The grade is against the *intended* change, confirmed by verification — it does not wait on phase 04.** Phase 02 grades what you plan to do (its intended reach and reversibility), and where more than one shape solves the task it grades the shape you intend to make, chosen with the smallest-reversible-change bias (the lowest-reach shape that fully solves — [smallest-reversible-change](smallest-reversible-change.md)). Where behavior-preservation can only be *confirmed* by a check that runs later — a dependency bump, a refactor with no current test — the phase-02 grade is **provisional on that intent**: grade on the increment's promise / the intended shape now, let [phase 03](../phases/03-make-the-change.md) act on it, and let phase 04 either confirm it or, if a surprise delta surfaces, escalate the tier and send the change back to [make-the-change](../phases/03-make-the-change.md). Grading never stalls waiting for the check; it commits to the intended-change grade and lets verification catch a wrong guess.

## Read these terms the same way

Two executors diverge when a word is left to taste; these are pinned:

- **edited unit** — the definitions the diff actually changes (a function, a class, a module's internals), *not* the whole file and *not* the whole commit.
- **"touched"** — a surface is touched when its *observable contract* changes so a consumer can tell, *not* merely when a line inside its definition was edited. A pure internal refactor that leaves a function's signature and behavior identical does not touch that function's contract, even though its body changed.
- **consumer set** — everything that observes the touched surface: callers, readers of the data/format it produces, and downstream jobs, services, or clients.
- **movable under your control** — you can update every consumer yourself in this change, either in the same diff or by a staged rollout you own; a consumer you cannot reach (external code, an unknown/non-enumerable set) or that crosses a deploy or version boundary (a separately-deploying service or a published library, *even in the same repo*) is **not** movable under your control.

## The three tiers

`(basis: reach ← Semantic Versioning 2.0.0 (PATCH/MINOR/MAJOR classify a change against a *declared* public API — item 1), widened by Hyrum's Law (all observable behavior of a used interface gets depended on, so the declared surface is reach's floor, not its ceiling). reversibility ← "Software Engineering at Google" ch. 22 on large-scale changes (a change that "cannot practically be submitted as a single atomic unit" is the escalation trigger; cheap-to-roll-back "cattle" is the low end). Actions ← direct is the high-reversibility/PATCH case; the guard is Feature Toggles (Hodgson) and Branch by Abstraction (Fowler/Humble); the migration path is Parallel Change / expand–contract (Fowler; "Refactoring Databases", Ambler & Sadalage, for the persisted-data archetype). SemVer *classifies* reach and is silent on rollout; the toggle/parallel-change literature *prescribes* the mechanism — the scale composes them, it does not choose between them. "blast radius" and "flag-day" are house vocabulary, not terms these authorities use.)`

`(basis: the tier boundaries are ratified by the maintainer, 2026-07-11, as a house standard — no authority sets a numeric bar on either axis (how many consumers, or what backout cost, tips one tier into the next), checked across SemVer, SE-at-Google ch. 22, Fowler's ParallelChange, and Hyrum's Law. Exactly where "external" begins, how heavily an internal surface must be consumed before Hyrum's Law promotes it, and what irreversibility forces a migration are the maintainer's ratified call — as review's severity scale is a house standard, since this scale has no CVSS to inherit boundaries from the way security-review's does.)`

- **contained** — behavior-preserving to every consumer and closed within the diff: nothing the diff leaves **unedited** observes a difference, and any caller the diff *does* touch takes only a **mechanical** update (a rename), not a logic change. Undone by a single clean revert. *Assignment test:* after the change, does anything outside what the diff edited observe a difference (per "touched")? No — **and** — did any consumer have to *adapt* its own logic rather than take a mechanical reference update? No — **and** — does one clean revert fully undo it? Yes. *Action:* **change it directly.**
  - *Anchor (bottom of scale):* renaming a private symbol whose few callers all take the mechanical rename in the same commit; a purely-internal refactor that leaves the observable contract and behavior unchanged. (A backward-compatible *bugfix* to non-published code is contained — but a PATCH released from a *published* library is **not**: its consumers are external, so it is `exposed` on reach. SemVer's rungs map onto reach only once you pin whether the change touches a public or an internal surface.)
- **bounded** — a change consumers **observe and must adapt to** (a contract or behavior change reaches them), where the consumer set is **enumerable and movable under your control** and crosses no deploy/version boundary; **or** a change reversible only via a runtime guard (live the instant it lands, so safe backout needs a flag / canary / branch-by-abstraction rather than a plain revert). *Action:* **stage it behind a guard** — or, when the full consumer set fits one atomic, reviewable diff, update every consumer in that same diff (atomic-vs-staged sets the *mechanism*, never the tier). The tell that separates this from `contained`: here a consumer had to *adapt*; in `contained` nothing outside the diff observes anything and any in-diff caller edit is mechanical.
  - *Anchor (middle):* changing an internal function's signature and updating its in-repo callers to match in one diff; a large internal refactor migrated caller-by-caller behind an abstraction; a user-facing behavior change shipped dark behind a flag and rolled out to a cohort.
- **exposed** — the consumer set is external, non-enumerable/unknown, or crosses a deploy or version boundary (so you cannot move all consumers under your control); **or** the change writes or destroys persisted data or state that cannot be un-written (a point of no return). *Action:* **require a migration path** — expand → migrate → contract, a deprecation window, or a versioned break ([preserve-the-contract](preserve-the-contract.md)), never a flag-day switch.
  - *Anchor (top of scale):* removing or renaming a public/exported function, or changing a wire/serialization format, that outside consumers depend on (a SemVer MAJOR); a schema migration that writes data readers depend on.

## The adjacent-tier discriminators

Walk down until a tier fits; the boundary tests stop a change sliding between rungs:

- **contained vs bounded** — after the change, does anything the diff left **unedited** observe a difference, **or** did any consumer have to **adapt** (change its own logic / handle new behavior) rather than take a mechanical in-diff reference update, **or** does backout need a guard rather than a clean revert? Any → bounded. None (behavior-preserving, closed within the diff, clean revert) → contained. The tell is *adaptation*: a mechanical rename of private callers stays contained; a signature or behavior change callers must accommodate is bounded, even when you update them all in one diff.
- **bounded vs exposed** — can you move the *entire* consumer set under your control (enumerable, no deploy/version boundary), **and** is the worst backout a guard (no data written)? Both yes → bounded. Is any consumer external/unknown/across a boundary, **or** is data already written? Either → exposed. (movability + irreversibility)

## Uncertainty resolves upward

The grader cannot observe a silent effect, and a risk graded low because its reach was invisible is the most expensive mistake in maintenance — the one [understand-blast-radius](../phases/02-understand-blast-radius.md) exists to prevent. So when you **cannot enumerate** the consumer set, **cannot prove** code is dead before deleting it, or **cannot rule out** a silent behavioral change you have no way to bound, the reach is *unknown* — and unknown reach grades **exposed**, not contained. Uncertainty about reach *or* about the behavioral delta resolves toward the higher tier — **unless a defined safeguard discharges it**: where a check that would catch the effect, a bounded contract, or (for a dependency upgrade) the semver increment plus the changelog-scan/green-checks/cooldown safeguards actually bound the delta, the uncertainty is *discharged*, not unknown (see *Grading a dependency upgrade* below). When two tiers seem to fit and the reach is *known*, the higher still wins unless you can name concretely why the reach is bounded; when the reach is genuinely *unknown and undischarged*, it stays high. Don't let an optimistic "probably fine" pull a change back down out of the max — but a safeguard that genuinely bounds the delta is not "probably fine," it is a discharged uncertainty.

## Grading a dependency upgrade

A dependency upgrade grades on the same two axes, but its *reach* needs one topology question answered first, and its *delta uncertainty* is discharged by a defined safeguard rather than resolving straight up.

- **Reach — who does the bump actually move?** The consumers of a dependency upgrade are: (a) *your own call sites* of the dependency — where a behavioral delta would land. Grade on the increment's promise and your intent, not on checks that haven't run yet: a backward-compatible (patch/minor) bump you are *not* adopting new features from is expected to leave the call sites unchanged → provisionally `contained` (nothing adapts); a bump whose changelog or your intent says the call sites must adapt → `bounded`. Phase 04's green checks then *confirm* that grade (no unexpected delta reached the call sites) or *escalate* it if a delta surfaces — the checks are the confirmation, never the grade-time input; (b) *your own exported contract* — in reach only if the dependency's changed behavior leaks through a surface you export; and (c) *co-importers of the same dependency* (a separate service or library that also imports it) — in reach **only if bumping your manifest/lockfile changes the version they resolve**. Resolve that topology before grading: under a shared or hoisted lockfile / workspace resolution, a co-importer *is* moved by your bump (and if it deploys separately or is external, that makes the change `exposed`); a co-importer holding its own isolated pin is **not** moved by your bump and is **not** in your reach. Don't auto-class every separately-deploying co-importer as exposed — first ask "does my bump change the version *it* resolves?" A co-importer *not* moved by your bump is not this change's reach and not its concern: surface "bump it too" as a follow-up only when a shared security/advisory fix motivates the co-bump, otherwise leave it on its own schedule (so a not-in-reach co-importer does not, by itself, turn the run into `partial-with-surfaced-followups`).
- **Delta uncertainty is discharged by the increment + safeguards, not resolved up.** You can't read all of upstream's source, so a naive reading of the uncertainty rule would grade *every* upgrade exposed — which is wrong. For a dependency upgrade the semver increment bounds the *expected* delta, and the safeguards ([dependency-upgrade-posture](dependency-upgrade-posture.md)) discharge the residual uncertainty. **Your own green checks are the load-bearing leg** — they test whether *your* behavior changed — with the changelog scan and the release cooldown corroborating; when a corroborating leg is unavailable (no scannable changelog, an unknown publish date), the discharge rests on the green checks, and for a purely-internal bump that is enough. So a **patch or minor** bump whose own checks pass grades `contained` or `bounded` (by its reach above) — **not** `exposed`. It escalates to `exposed` only when the delta is genuinely un-bounded: a **major/breaking or pre-1.0** increment; a bump to a surface you **export** or to **persisted data** whose changelog and checks can't rule out a change there (this trigger applies only when the change actually has such an exported/persisted surface — a purely-internal bump does not); or a co-importer your bump moves that deploys separately. A cleared check is a discharged uncertainty; a major/pre-1.0 increment is an un-dischargeable one.
