# Surface disagreement

When sources genuinely conflict, the honest answer represents the conflict — it doesn't pick the more fluent side, or average two positions into a compromise neither source holds. Collapsing a live disagreement into a single confident answer is a fabrication: it asserts a consensus that doesn't exist, and a blended "sources broadly agree" destroys exactly the information the caller needs to decide.

## Is it a real conflict?

1. **Run the conflict test before calling anything a conflict.** Two sources genuinely conflict only when they answer the *same question, at the same scope and version*, such that satisfying one violates the other. Otherwise it isn't a conflict: one being stricter (meeting it satisfies both) is **emphasis**; a different scope or version is **applicability**; a newer document replacing an older one is **supersession**. Report the relationship, not a manufactured conflict.

## Surface, don't collapse

2. **Locate where the dispute actually lives.** Sources rarely disagree about everything; find the specific point of divergence — a definition, a boundary condition, a dataset, a version — and state it. "A and B disagree on whether this holds above 10k rows," not "sources are mixed."
3. **Resolve only what the evidence resolves.** A conflict resolves when one side is clearly stronger, or newer where the field has moved — then say so and why. When neither dominates, both positions stand, each as its own item: who takes it (with its source's strength), what it requires, its basis, and the tradeoff a follower accepts. The claim grades **contested** on the support scale [results-and-certainty](results-and-certainty.md) defines, never established, and the weaker position is never dropped to make the conflict disappear.
4. **Never manufacture the middle.** Two incompatible claims don't average into a true one; if A says "always" and B says "never," the answer is "contested — A says …, B says …," not "sometimes."
5. **A project-reality-vs-norm divergence is always a finding.** When the code or its history disagrees with a spec, the documentation or the literature, surface the divergence and never reconcile it: the caller decides whether the project is wrong or the norm doesn't apply here.

(basis: the authoritative-literature explorer's "genuine conflict vs difference in emphasis" test)
