This is the anti-stale-instruction rule, and it's the difference between a procedure that ages well and one that quietly rots. A codified skill outlives the moment it was written: the system around it changes, names move, thresholds shift, sources get reorganized. Anything you bake in that depends on the *present state* of that system becomes a lie the day the state changes — and a lie in a procedure is worse than a gap, because the executor follows it confidently. The rule: encode the durable **method** and the **why** behind it; refuse to bake in a fact that exploration can re-derive.

A method is a way of finding or deciding; a fact is the current answer. *"Start the audit from the plugin's most recently changed skills"* is a method — it stays true however the plugin evolves. *"Skill X is the newest in the plugin"* is a fact — true today, stale after the next release, and exactly the kind of thing phase 02's explorers can re-derive on demand. Codify the method that *finds* the most recently changed skills; never freeze yesterday's answer into the procedure. The same goes for anything with a name, a number, or a location that the surrounding system controls: codify *how to look it up*, not the value you looked up.

The why matters as much as the method, because the why is what lets a cold executor adapt the method to a case you didn't foresee. A step that says *what* to do but not *why* breaks the moment reality differs from the example in the author's head; a step that carries its reason lets the executor reason from it. Carry the rationale — *"start from the most recently changed **because** breakage concentrates where the last edits landed"* — so the executor can extend the method rather than only mimic it.

The test to run on every line you're about to commit: **will this still be true after the system changes?** If yes, it's method — keep it. If no, it's a fact with a shelf life — push it out of the procedure and replace it with the method that re-derives it. A fact that drifts in six months is a liability sitting in a file no one re-reads; the procedure that knows how to find the current value never goes stale.

This rule binds codify's own content too. anvil's skills are authored to the standard they enforce, so a codified procedure that hard-codes a fact fails the very check this rule names — and the maintainer inherits a skill that was wrong before it was even run.
