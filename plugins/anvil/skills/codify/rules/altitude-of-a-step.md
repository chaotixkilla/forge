Altitude is two things at once — *how big* a step is and *how abstract* it's phrased — and a step that's wrong on either axis breaks the procedure differently. Too fine-grained and the procedure drowns the executor in trivia and loses its shape; too coarse and a "step" is really a project the executor still has to plan. Too concrete and the step names a tool, pinning the procedure to one vendor and leaking exactly what the kit's audits exist to catch; too abstract and it's a slogan the executor can't act on. Getting altitude right is what makes a step *runnable* — actionable, bounded, and portable.

On granularity: a phase is a **step** — not a single throwaway sentence, and not a sub-project. The test cuts both ways. If a step needs its own ordered sub-steps before an executor could run it, it's secretly a phase of its own — promote it and decompose it. If a step is too slight to stand on its own — a single mechanical move with no decision in it — fold it into the neighbor it serves. The right grain is the level at which a competent executor reads the step and knows what to do next without either re-planning it or tripping over it. Match the grain of the target plugin's existing steps, too, so a new step reads like its siblings rather than a transplant from a finer or coarser skill — but matching governs grain and voice only, never which slots exist or what standards the steps enforce; a sibling known to be under-built is a warning to note, not a precedent to copy.

On abstraction, the hard rule: **never name a concrete tool, vendor, CLI, service, or MCP id in a step.** A step names the *capability* it needs; the concrete implementation lives in an adapter below the skill layer, swappable without touching the step. *"Open a change request against the target"* is a capability; naming the specific forge that hosts the change request is a tool-leak. The discriminating test: *if this step can only be said by naming a tool, the tool belongs in an adapter* — and the step should be rewritten to name what it needs that tool *for*. Drawn right, swapping the underlying tool changes an adapter and leaves every step untouched; that invariance is the proof the altitude is correct.

The too-abstract failure has its own test. A step is actionable when the executor can begin it without inventing policy the step doesn't give; if acting first requires deciding a bar, criterion, or threshold the step omits, it's a slogan wearing an imperative — the missing decision goes back through the resolve pass, not into looser phrasing. The capability altitude is high on *tools*, never high on *decisions*: a step may leave open which tool does the work, but not what counts as done.

The anti-pattern is the "helpful specific" — reaching for a concrete tool name because it feels clearer to the reader. It is clearer, briefly, and it's a trap: it couples the procedure to one stack and fails the same tool-leak check anvil runs on every plugin it audits. Codify's output is held to that standard like any other, so a leaked tool name in a codified step is a defect the kit will flag in its own work. Name the capability; let the adapter name the tool.
