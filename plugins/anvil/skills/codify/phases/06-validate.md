A procedure that reads correctly to its author is not yet proven, because the author carries the whole conversation in their head — every inference, every "obviously," every fork they resolved silently while typing. The cold executor carries none of that. Validation is the act of stepping outside the conversation and asking two questions in order. The floor: *could a fresh agent, with just this skill and a real input, run it to the end without guessing?* The bar: **convergence** — *would two independent cold executors, given only these files, make the same judgments and produce output of the same character?* A procedure can clear the floor and still fail the bar: every spot where a generic executor wouldn't stall but would silently fill a gap with average practice is a hole, even though the run completes. Where either answer is no, you've found a hole the conversation was papering over — and the fix is to loop back to the phase that owns it, not to patch over the symptom here.

## Dry-run it as a fresh executor

Take a real example input and walk the codification as if you'd never seen this conversation — recruit the **cold-executor critic** to do exactly this (or, without fan-out, run the cold walk yourself in a deliberately amnesiac pass): a fresh agent that assumes nothing from context and runs the procedure against the example, stalling wherever a step leaves it underspecified. Every stall is a finding. A stall on a fork that wasn't enumerated routes back to phase 04; a stall on a vague adjective routes back to phase 04's quantify step; a stall on "which tool do I even use" routes back to phase 05's altitude pass; a missing input routes back to phase 03. Validation doesn't fix holes — it locates them and sends them home to the phase that resolves them.

## Read the page for the open standards

Stalls are the easy findings; the dangerous ones don't stall — the run completes and two executors' outputs merely differ. These live on the page, not in the run, so recruit the **standards-skeptic critic** to read the drafted files for them (or, without fan-out, apply its written definition to the files yourself in the same amnesiac pass). It hunts the **silent fills** — spots where a generic executor wouldn't stop, just quietly substitute average practice — and treats each as blocker-class, same as a stall: a judgment word at a decision point with no reachable discriminator (*good, significant, appropriate*); a grading, ranking, or selection verb whose discriminator is stated neither inline nor in a cited rule; rated, scored, or ranked output missing its scale, **a one-clause definition on any named level**, its assignment discriminators, or its anchors; **a pinned bar carrying neither (basis: …) nor (routed to maintainer: …)**; a selection or filter step with no stated criterion; an output specified as a field list with no shape example — two executors will format it two ways; an open bar with no recorded open-by-design reason. These route to phase 04's standard-closure step; a form or phrasing issue routes to phase 05.

## Push every inference into a surfaced assumption

Anything the procedure relies on that came from *conversation memory* rather than from the skill text is invisible to the cold executor — and therefore a latent failure. Sweep them out: each thing you "just knew" becomes an explicit, written assumption the maintainer can read and override. *"This assumes the source material is complete before the run starts"* is far safer than the same fact living only in your head. The phase 02 inferences you labeled along the way collect here into the handoff's assumption list.

## Push every unresolved fork into an open question

Where a genuine unknown survived all of sourcing and resolution — a decision the maintainer must make that nothing could settle for them — flag it as an open question rather than silently picking. A surfaced open question is a decision handed to the right owner; a silent guess is a bug with the author's name on it. Distinguish the two honestly: an assumption is a likely answer you've taken and labeled; an open question is a fork you genuinely cannot resolve and won't pretend to.

## The gate: nothing ships with an open standard

This phase is a gate, not a review — the build is not done until both lenses have been applied to the drafted files and every finding they raise is closed. The pass condition is mechanical, checkable by inspection, and non-negotiable: **zero open standard-points remain** (each one pinned or open-by-design with its reason on the page); **every pinned bar carries its marker** — a (basis: …) or a (routed to maintainer: …); **every named scale level carries a definition**, with the scale's discriminators and anchors present; **every grading, ranking, or selection verb reaches its discriminator**; and the cold-executor's example run completes with no unresolved stall. Fail any clause and the draft loops back to the phase that owns the hole — it does not ship because it reads finished. And closure is not one-shot: editing a file to close a finding *changes content*, and the change can open a fresh standard-point the first pass never saw — pinning a bar can introduce a scale that now needs defining; resolving a fork can add a branch carrying its own judgment word; supplying a missing output can outrun the shape the output was pinned to. So the gate is a **clean re-run, not a first-pass tally**: after closing a pass's findings, re-apply both lenses to the *changed* files and keep looping until a pass over the current draft raises nothing — where "nothing" means each lens comes back clean *on its own terms*, not merely *different*. And when a fix mints a *new determinant* — a distinction or branch condition that more than one file classifies on — the fix is not localized to the file you edited: carry the new distinction to every file that branches on it before re-verifying, or the loop rediscovers the omission one file per round while those files classify the same run two different ways. A partial fix can leave a residue the **same** lens re-flags next round: cutting a speculative build-out leaves a boundary note, then a generalized clause — one skeptic peeling a single seam over successive passes — a distinct shape from a fix that opens a *new* divergence, and just as unfinished until that lens returns clean. And when the *same* standard-point re-opens across **two or more** re-verify rounds, stop patching it: a bar that keeps re-opening under successive fixes is structurally ambiguous — one passage silently conflating two dimensions that each need their own test — and each added clause only exposes the next edge. Decompose the passage into named sub-checks, one per dimension, rather than adding another clause to the overloaded sentence; that converges it where patching cannot. A finding closed in isolation, its fix never re-checked, is how the second divergence ships. "Looks convergent" is not a pass; the clauses above are.

## Let --verify set the depth above the gate

The closure gate above always holds; `--verify` tunes how deep the *cold-executor convergence walk* runs on top of it, because that walk's cost is what varies with stakes:

- `--verify=off` — skip the cold-executor dry-run. The standards-skeptic closure gate still runs — it's cheap inspection, and an open standard is exactly the defect that ships silently under a hand review.
- `--verify=light` (the working default) — run the dry-run, then surface the assumptions, the open questions, and any silent-fill spots found, and proceed. The maintainer gets the procedure plus a visible list of what it assumed, what's still open, and where it would diverge.
- `--verify=strict` — additionally block the handoff while *any* fork that changes the output stays open, beyond the standard-points the gate already blocks on. Nothing ships until every output-affecting unknown is resolved, pinned with its marker, or consciously accepted by the maintainer. This is the bar for a procedure that will run unattended.

## Finish: hand off the whole change set

Report what you wrote and what you assumed as one coherent handoff: the phase files (and any rule files) authored into the target skill, the surfaced assumptions, the open questions, and the bars you pinned with their markers — every one routed to the maintainer flagged as such — the maintainer ratifies a proposed standard far faster than they can excavate a silent one. Deliver the procedure *and* its caveats together. A procedure delivered without its assumption list looks more finished than it is, which is the most dangerous way to be wrong.
