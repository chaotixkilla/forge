The shell exists but the plugin can't yet do anything — `skills/` is an empty folder. This phase seeds the pool, and it does so by *delegating*, not by writing skills inline. new-plugin owns the marketplace-level act of birthing a plugin; it does not own the craft of authoring a skill. That craft is two other skills' job, and the split between them is the same scaffold-before-content discipline the whole kit is built on: lay every skill's structure first, fill the method after.

## Delegate the skeleton to scaffold-skill

For each capability the pool design earned in phase 1, hand off to `scaffold-skill --plugin=<name>` to lay that skill's slot skeleton: its frontmatter (the capability as the description, the flags it needs, and — only if the plugin is config-bearing — its config prerequisites) plus the slot folders it requires. scaffold-skill is where the per-skill altitude phrasing and slot choices are made; new-plugin's job is only to point it at each designed skill in turn and pass the plugin context through. Don't reproduce scaffold-skill's logic here — call it.

Work breadth-first, the way the kit builds everything: scaffold the skeletons across *all* the seeded skills before filling any one of them. Structural mistakes — a mis-named skill, a slot that doesn't earn its place — are cheap to fix while everything is still a one-line stub and expensive once method sits on top. Lay the whole skeleton, see it as a whole, and only then fill.

## Hand the method to codify

Once a skill's skeleton exists, its procedure is empty. Hand that skill to `codify` to fill the method from the maintainer's process — the content half of authoring. codify is the kit's own engine for turning a human process into a runnable procedure (split goal from process, source the knowledge, decompose the skeleton, resolve decisions, classify fidelity, validate); it fills the skeleton scaffold-skill produced. new-plugin doesn't author procedure prose — it routes each skeleton to codify and lets that skill do the work.

How far to drive codify at birth has a floor, a discriminator, and a deliberately open top. The **floor**: every scaffolded skill leaves birth with at least codify's goal/process split done — if even that split can't be made, the skill wasn't ready to scaffold; send it back to the deferred list. Past the floor, the **discriminator** is whether the process already exists: if the maintainer can narrate the skill's method end-to-end today, drive codify to a full validated procedure — stopping short would just defer transcription; if the process is still forming (decision points the maintainer can't yet resolve), stop at the first-pass split and pause for steering — forcing completeness there manufactures guesses that read as decisions. How many of the pool's skills get the full drive at birth is deliberately open: it depends on which of the maintainer's processes are settled, which no rule can know in advance. A half-designed skill should not block the plugin's birth.

## Seed only what the design earned

Resist the pull to fill out the whole imagined pool now. Seed the skills phase 1's design actually justified and the skeptic let through; leave the rest as named-but-unbuilt entries in the design, to be added on demand later via the same scaffold-skill → codify path. Seeds, not specs: a lean plugin you grow as needs arrive beats a wide one stuffed with speculative skills that the contract and packaging audits will later have to reckon with. If a skill can't yet justify a single concrete responsibility, it's not ready to be scaffolded — note it and move on.
