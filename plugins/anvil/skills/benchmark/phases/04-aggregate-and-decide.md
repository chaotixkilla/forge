Phase 03 handed back a pile: for every scenario, the graded repeats of the baseline arm and the graded repeats of the changed arm. A pile of grades is not a decision. This phase collapses it into one classification per scenario — improvement, regression, or within-noise — and into the single blocking question the verdict turns on: did anything get worse? This is where benchmark earns its cost. A single dogfood pass or a static critic can tell you the changed tree runs and reads well; only an arm-versus-arm comparison with repeats can catch the trade where a change fixes one scenario and quietly breaks another. That trade is invisible to a one-shot run and invisible to a mean. Finding it is the whole point, so the aggregation below is built to surface it, never to smooth it over.

## Reduce each arm before you compare arms

Resist the urge to jump straight to "changed beat baseline." First reduce each arm on its own. Per scenario, per arm, you hold the graded repeats phase 02 produced (their count is `--repeats`, defaulted to three in [signal-vs-noise](../rules/signal-vs-noise.md)), each already a band on the fixed four-band ladder phase 03 pinned. Collapse those repeats into two things: a central outcome — the representative band for that arm on that scenario — and the run-to-run spread across the repeats. The reduction is pinned here, in concrete arithmetic, precisely so two runs of benchmark over the same logs land on the same numbers; do not substitute a reduction of your own.

Read the four bands as an ordered scale and give them ranks so the arithmetic has something to bite on: **broken = 0, degraded = 1, minor = 2, clean = 3** — worse at the bottom, so a positive delta between arms will read as an improvement. Treat these as ranks, never as scores: the gaps between adjacent bands are not claimed to be equal, so every reduction below uses only their order, never an arithmetic average of them. The **central outcome is the median band** of an arm's repeats — sort the repeats by rank and take the middle one; with the default three repeats that is simply the middle of the three, and on an even count take the worse (lower-rank) of the two middle bands, so the reduction never rounds an arm toward its flattering side. Median rather than mean for two reasons: the scale is ordinal, where a mean would invent distances the bands don't carry, and a single fluke repeat should not drag the center off the band the arm actually keeps landing on. The **run-to-run spread is the observed range in rank steps** — the lowest repeat rank subtracted from the highest for that arm. Because it is a range, a file-rooted finding (phase 03's constant offset, present in every repeat of the scenarios that exercise its file) shifts those repeats by the same amount and cancels out of it untouched; only run-rooted variation — the jitter repeats exist to measure — widens the spread. That is exactly the separation phase 03's file-rooted-versus-run-rooted tag was kept for: the constant is not averaged as though it wandered.

The spread is not a nuisance to average away — it is the measuring stick for everything that follows. A version that scores brilliantly on one repeat and poorly on the next two is not a good version; it is an unreliable one, and the spread is the only place that unreliability gets recorded. An arm with a wide spread has told you something real: its behavior on that scenario is not yet stable enough to grade against.

Only once both arms are reduced independently do you compute the per-scenario delta — the changed arm's central band minus the baseline's, in those same rank steps. A positive delta means the changed arm centered on a cleaner band, a negative delta on a worse one. The delta is a per-scenario quantity, never one number for the whole run. Collapsing every scenario into a single grand average is precisely the move this phase exists to prevent, and it is prevented by keeping the delta scenario-local from the start.

## Classify against the spread, not against zero

Three buckets, one per scenario: improvement, regression, within-noise. The classifier is not "is the delta positive?" — it is "is the delta large enough to be told apart from this scenario's own jitter?" [signal-vs-noise](../rules/signal-vs-noise.md) pins why the bar is the spread itself and never an absolute count of band-steps, and why measuring against each arm's own scatter is self-calibrating; this phase applies that principle on the ordinal ladder as concrete arithmetic. Take the **wider of the two arms' spreads** — the noisier arm sets the bar, because a center gap smaller than that arm's own run-to-run wander could be nothing but that wander. The delta counts as signal only when its magnitude **strictly exceeds** that wider spread: `|delta| > max(spread_baseline, spread_changed)`, both sides in band-steps. Clear the bar and the delta's sign decides direction; fail to clear it and the scenario is within-noise. One guard runs before you file anything within-noise, and it is the next paragraph: a spread wide enough that no delta could ever clear it is inconclusive, not settled.

A delta smaller than the spread is within-noise. Report it as no measurable change — flatly, with no spin. A delta you cannot distinguish from run-to-run jitter is not evidence of improvement, and dressing a sub-noise wobble as a win is the single most abused move in A/B work; the bar exists to close exactly that door. Only a delta that clears the wider spread earns a directional label, and its sign chooses which: cleared upward is an improvement, cleared downward is a regression.

One distinction the spread forces, and it matters, so it is pinned to a concrete trigger: a scenario whose **wider spread reaches two band-steps or more cannot resolve an ordinary difference at all**. The ladder tops out at three ranks, so once an arm wanders two full bands run to run, the only delta that could ever exceed that spread is the maximal three — centers pinned at broken against clean — and short of that corner every result collapses to within-noise no matter what the change did. That is why two steps is the cut: it is the point past which the scenario has lost the power to answer the question, not a point at which it happens to answer "no." Do not file such a scenario as within-noise, which reads as a settled "nothing happened." File it as **inconclusive** and route it back — raise `--repeats` for that scenario, or tighten the scenario per [scenario-corpus-discipline](../rules/scenario-corpus-discipline.md). The same holds when both arms center on the grading floor — a median band of broken on each: both failing is not parity, it is a scenario that discriminates nothing, and it owes the corpus a fix rather than the verdict a data point.

## Regressions lead, and the mean never buries one

Any scenario classified as a regression is the most important output of this phase, so it never gets averaged into a grand mean. Averaging the per-scenario deltas is exactly how a change that fixes three scenarios by a little while breaking one by a lot comes out net-positive — the fix-A-break-B trade laundered into a passing number. Phase 04 refuses that at the source: it keeps every delta scenario-local (above) and emits, alongside the per-scenario record, the one blocking fact the verdict turns on — whether any **confirmed regression** exists, meaning a per-scenario delta that cleared the signal bar in the worse direction. What that fact *does* to the verdict — that a single confirmed regression blocks net-improvement, why, and whether a human or calling skill may consciously accept the trade — is phase 05's rule to state and apply; this phase's job is to surface the fact, ranked first, so phase 05 cannot miss it. Root-causing a regression and judging a trade acceptable are phase 05's and the caller's moves, not this phase's — phase 04 emits the raw fact that a confirmed regression exists, nothing more.

## Hand the decision to the report

This phase decides; phase 05 renders. Emit a per-scenario record — the scenario, each arm's central band, each arm's spread in band-steps, the delta in band-steps, and the classification (improvement, regression, within-noise, or inconclusive) — plus the one blocking fact the verdict turns on: whether any confirmed regression exists. Order the record so regressions come first, then within-noise and inconclusive, then improvements; the reporting phase presents in that order, and fixing the order here means it cannot be lost downstream. Do not write the final verdict prose or honor `--report` here — that is phase 05's work. This phase's contract is narrower and non-negotiable: every scenario carries a classification backed by its own spread, and no regression is ever folded into an average.
