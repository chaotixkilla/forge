A benchmark run that ends in a headline number — "the changed arm scored higher" — hands the caller a fact with no decision inside it. The run and grading phases produced something better: a per-scenario picture of where the change helped, where it hurt, and where the two arms are indistinguishable. This phase turns that picture into a report the caller can act on, then into a single verdict they can execute. The ordering is not cosmetic. The one thing benchmark exists to catch — a change that fixes one scenario while silently breaking another — is invisible to every static critic and to a single run; it only shows up as a per-scenario divergence in an A/B with repeats. Bury it under a longer list of improvements and the whole comparison was wasted. So: report the bad news first, give every finding a file to fix it in, and close with a call the caller can act on without re-reading the logs.

## Rank the outcomes — regressions first, then within-noise and inconclusive, then improvements

The [aggregate-and-decide phase (04)](04-aggregate-and-decide.md) handed you each scenario already classified — regression, within-noise, inconclusive, or improvement — with the per-arm spread that earned the label. Emit them in exactly the order phase 04 fixed, so the two phases never diverge on it: **regressions, then within-noise and inconclusive, then improvements.** The order encodes the skill's reason for existing. A caller who reads a regression on line one holds the change before it ships; a caller who has to scroll past a list of wins to reach it may not — so regressions lead, and the improvements, real as they are, come last, never allowed to bury the one finding this tier was built to surface. Between them sit the scenarios that produced no actionable finding: **within-noise**, where the arms could not be told apart, and **inconclusive**, where the spread was too wide to resolve or both arms sat at the grading floor. Both are reported for completeness and for the honesty of the verdict — an inconclusive scenario in particular is an unmeasured surface the caller must weigh, per [signal-vs-noise](../rules/signal-vs-noise.md) — but neither is ranked among the things to act on.

Inside the regression and improvement bands, rank by the **magnitude of the confirmed delta** — worst regression first, largest improvement first — where "confirmed" means the delta cleared the signal bar in [signal-vs-noise](../rules/signal-vs-noise.md); a delta inside the noise band is not a regression however unwelcome its direction, and belongs in the within-noise band.

## Give every finding an address

A regression the caller can't locate is an alarm with no address — true, maybe, but not yet fixable. So every regression and every improvement points back to the file(s) in the change under test that plausibly own the delta. Trace it: the paired arm logs show *where* on the scenario the changed arm diverged from the baseline arm, the grading phase's critic tells you *why* that divergence graded worse (or better), and the diff tells you *which touched file* sits under that point. Name it — the phase step, rule, or wiring seam the change altered — so the fix has somewhere to go.

Be honest when attribution can't resolve to one file. A diff that touches several files, exercised by one scenario, may not let the evidence isolate a single culprit; name the candidates and say the evidence is shared, rather than manufacturing a lone offender. And a regression whose cause cannot be found in the diff at all is not a free-floating complaint to ship — it is a signal to suspect the grading or the noise band itself. Send it back to re-check before it goes in the report as a regression; a false regression that halts a good change costs as much as a missed one that ships a bad change.

## Emit each finding in one fixed shape

Reports converge across runs only when their shape is pinned, so emit every finding in the same shape — classification, scenario, the per-arm result with its spread, the file pointer, and the evidence:

> **[regression]** scenario `edit-with-open-bar` — changed arm graded *degraded* in 3/3 runs, baseline graded *clean* in 3/3 (delta clears the signal bar) → `phases/03` step 2, the reworded grading step under test → the standards-skeptic flags the changed wording as leaving the bar open where the baseline pinned it; the fix-A here broke this B.

The per-arm result always carries its spread, not just a point value — "3/3 vs 3/3" or "mean and range across repeats" — because the spread is what separates a real delta from run-to-run jitter, and a reader must be able to re-derive the classification from the line. For the subjective residue no critic could grade objectively, state the outcome with the judge's recorded basis rather than a manufactured number, per [grading-the-ungradable](../rules/grading-the-ungradable.md) — an honest "the judge preferred the changed arm's phrasing, basis recorded" beats a false precision the comparison can't support.

## Land a single verdict

Close with one label the caller can act on — **net-improvement**, **net-regression**, or **no-measurable-change** — composed from the ranked outcomes by a fixed rule, not by feel:

- **net-regression** if **one or more** scenarios confirmed a regression — regardless of how many improved.
- **net-improvement** if **zero** confirmed regressions, **zero** inconclusive scenarios, **and at least one** confirmed improvement.
- **no-measurable-change** otherwise — no scenario confirmed a regression, and either every scenario landed within-noise (no delta cleared the signal bar in either direction) or one or more scenarios stayed inconclusive, leaving an unresolved surface that no improvement elsewhere can certify away.

The tolerance for confirmed regressions is pinned at **zero**, and the reason is the killer feature itself: the fix-A-break-B trade is exactly what this tier exists to surface, so a rule that let a count of improvements outvote a single real regression would blind the caller to the highest-value catch benchmark makes. The improvements are not erased — they are reported, ranked below the within-noise and inconclusive bands — and a human or calling skill may consciously accept the trade. But the default recommendation treats any real regression as disqualifying, because averaging it away is the one failure this whole heavyweight tier was built to prevent.

An inconclusive scenario is held to the same conservatism, and for the same reason. It is a surface the run could not measure — the spread never resolved, or both arms sat at the floor — and an unmeasured surface is exactly where a fix-A-break-B trade can hide. So a single inconclusive scenario blocks **net-improvement** the way a regression does, but it does not manufacture a **net-regression** it never confirmed; the verdict falls to **no-measurable-change** instead. This effect is pinned, not weighed per run: the tier's whole value is refusing to certify a change as better while a scenario it might have broken is still unresolved, so one improvement — or ten — cannot outvote one scenario the run could not read. The escape is not to relax the rule but to resolve the scenario — raise `--repeats` or tighten it per [scenario-corpus-discipline](../rules/scenario-corpus-discipline.md), then re-run — exactly the route phase 04 attached when it minted the inconclusive label.

State the verdict honestly about its own variance. A verdict resting on deltas that only just cleared the signal bar, or on the pinned-minimum repeat count from [signal-vs-noise](../rules/signal-vs-noise.md), is a weaker call than one resting on wide, stable separations — say which it is. And read a spread of zero for what it is: *no variance the run could resolve at the band's granularity*, not a proof of stability — when every arm's spread is zero, report the verdict as what [signal-vs-noise](../rules/signal-vs-noise.md) calls a comparative-audit result, not as a confidence the repeats never earned. And read **no-measurable-change** for what it is: not "the change is worthless," but one of two honest admissions the caller must be able to tell apart. When every scenario landed within-noise it means "at this repeat count and this corpus, benchmark could not distinguish the arms" — often because the scenarios never exercised the changed surface; point the caller at [scenario-corpus-discipline](../rules/scenario-corpus-discipline.md) to widen the corpus or at [signal-vs-noise](../rules/signal-vs-noise.md) to raise the repeats. When instead a scenario stayed inconclusive, the verdict is blocked rather than null — improvements may be real, but one surface went unmeasured — and the caller's move is to resolve that scenario and re-run before reading the result as a clean bill. Name which of the two you are handing back, rather than letting either masquerade as a clean bill. Finally, a scenario the baseline arm could not run at all — because it exercises a surface the change newly introduced — has no A/B to compare; report it as a new-surface outcome graded on the changed arm alone, flagged not-comparable, and keep it out of the within-noise count so it never dilutes the verdict.

## Honor `--report`

Return the ranked findings and the verdict inline by default — prose back to the caller, regressions first, verdict last. Under `--report=artifact`, render the identical content as a structured page via the configured artifacts backend (or a local file), so a costly multi-arm, multi-repeat run leaves a durable, scannable record instead of a wall of inline text. The content does not change across formats; only the delivery does. The example names the *capability* — the configured artifacts backend — deliberately, not a concrete renderer: which backend produces the page is a wiring concern, not this phase's.

## The verdict is the hand-back

Who reads the verdict decides what it is. Invoked as a gate by revise or release, the verdict is the machine hand-back: **net-regression** tells the caller to hold or revert, and the ranked regressions travel with it as a worklist — each already carrying the file pointer and route the caller's repair engine needs, exactly as a dogfood finding does. **net-improvement** or **no-measurable-change** clears the gate, the latter with the caveat that nothing was measured, not that nothing changed. Invoked directly by a human, the verdict is the answer to the question that justified paying for this tier — "is this change actually better?" — and the honest answer is the label *plus the trade beneath it*: what improved, what it cost, and how confident the separation was. Either way, the verdict is not the whole report — it is the one line the caller acts on, standing on the ranked evidence above it.
